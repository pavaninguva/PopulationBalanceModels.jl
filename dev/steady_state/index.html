Stationary population balances

A continuous crystallizer can operate at a constant size distribution even though crystals continue to nucleate, grow and leave the vessel. Its stationary equations balance those rates directly. There is no time span or physical initial condition: the nonlinear solver needs a numerical guess for the unknown state.

For a well-mixed vessel with residence time $\tau$, constant growth $G>0$ and boundary nucleation $J$, the model is

\[\frac{d}{dL}\left(Gn(L)\right)+\frac{n(L)}{\tau}=0, \qquad G n(0)=J.\]

On the nonnegative half-line its analytical solution and moments are

\[n(L)=\frac{J}{G}\exp\left(-\frac{L}{G\tau}\right), \qquad M_k=k!\,J G^k\tau^{k+1}.\]

Here $n$ is number per process volume per size; $J$ is number per process volume per time. The package's sectional unknown remains the integral of $n$ over each bin. Particle withdrawal is Dilution, not an extra size-boundary condition. A finite computational upper boundary introduces an additional escape flux, which must be included in the number balance.

Define and solve

Load the optional NonlinearSolve dependency in your working environment. The physical declaration and spatial methods are the same ones used for a transient calculation.

using PopulationBalanceModels, NonlinearSolve

grid = Grid1D(range(0,12; length=161); coordinate=:length)
model = PBMModel(
    growth = Growth((L,p,t,ctx) -> p.G),
    births = Nucleation((p,t,ctx) -> p.J, 0.0),
    withdrawal = Dilution((L,p,t,ctx) -> 1/p.tau),
)
op = compile(model, grid;
    methods=TermMethods(nucleation=BoundaryInjection()),
    bc=SizeBC(NoFlux(), OpenBoundary()))
problem = StationaryPBMProblem(op, zeros(length(grid));
    p=(G=0.4,J=1.2,tau=2.0), autonomous=true)
solution = solve_stationary(problem) # RobustMultiNewton()
solution.converged || error("stationary solve failed: $(solution.retcode)")
bin_density = density(grid, solution.u)
diagnostics(solution)

autonomous=true declares that all callbacks, fields and inlet conditions are time-independent. The package cannot prove that property for arbitrary Julia callbacks. Alternatively, time=t explicitly freezes prescribed conditions at one time. That calculation does not describe the response to a changing history. Neither choice inserts physical time integration.

solve_stationary(problem, algorithm; abstol, reltol, maxiters, ...) accepts a NonlinearSolve algorithm. nonlinearproblem(problem) exposes the same problem to that library directly, with flattened physical unknowns. Use solve_stationary when you want the package's independent final checks and named result accessors. Ordinary callback, closure and coefficient restrictions remain in force. Invalid trial states raise an error; no negative values are clipped. RobustMultiNewton does not itself guarantee nonnegative or realizable iterates. For quadrature moment families the default uses finite-difference Jacobians, because inversion eigensystems need not support dual numbers. Sectional and exact-moment residuals retain NonlinearSolve's default differentiation. An explicit algorithm controls its own differentiation backend; the physical closure is unchanged.

Coupling, constraints and scaling

For a PBMSystem, supply named population guesses and named bulk guesses:

problem = StationaryPBMProblem(system, (crystals=population_guess,);
    bulk=(c=concentration_guess,), p=parameters, autonomous=true)
solution = solve_stationary(problem)
population(solution, :crystals)
bulk(solution, :c)

All physical residuals are assembled together, including the usual same-state bulk callbacks and selected-mechanism exchanges. Do not couple solute uptake to the total stationary population derivative, which is zero: growth and births can consume solute while withdrawal removes the resulting crystals. ConservativeExchange and MomentExchange retain that distinction.

Native cumulative loss ledgers are historical integrals and are excluded from the stationary unknowns. accounting_rates(solution) instead returns removal rates, including nonzero escape at a stationary distribution. Physical reservoirs with their own balances remain unknowns. User-defined passive counters must be removed from the stationary model; the package cannot infer their role.

For a coupled axial reactor, axial_problem(problem, domain; axial_scheme=Upwind()) accepts a local stationary system directly. Supply guess for a spatially varying state or let it repeat the local guess in every axial cell. Its inlet and fields must obey the same autonomy/frozen-condition declaration. This uses the existing axial flux discretization without a fabricated transient time span.

Closed conservative populations often have a family of stationary states. Supply a conserved inventory by replacing a redundant equation, for example constraints=(1 => (u,p,t)->sum(u)/target_number-1,) for one closed population. The callback returns a dimensionless constraint and receives the state in its ordinary shape. Rows use linear physical-state indices. The solver also checks every original physical balance, so replacing an independent equation cannot conceal an unbalanced source. Use named population/bulk accessors in callbacks for coupled systems instead of assuming offsets.

residual_scale is a positive scalar or state-shaped array of characteristic physical rates. Physical residuals are divided by it; constraint callbacks are already dimensionless. Choose scales to make the requested tolerance meaningful across population and bulk units. A result is accepted only when the backend reports success and both constrained and original scaled residuals satisfy abstol + reltol*r0, with r0 the initial constrained infinity norm. The default reltol=0 makes this an absolute scaled-balance test.

Observe and estimate parameters

Observation operators apply to the converged state, with no invented time history. For a coupled sectional system, for example, observe(PopulationMoment(:crystals, 0), solution) returns number concentration and observe(BulkObservable(:c), solution) returns dissolved concentration. For an axial system, wrap a local observation in CellObservable(BulkObservable(:c), outlet_cell).

Use the same Bayesian estimation API as for transient models. Omit timestamps when declaring stationary data:

measurement = Measurement([observed_number], MomentObservable(0);
    likelihood=GaussianLikelihood(sigma=number_sd))
experiment = InferenceExperiment(:vessel, problem, measurement;
    solver=StationarySolver(abstol=1e-12))
parameter = ParameterSpec(:J; initial=1.0, transform=LogTransform(),
    prior=Prior(Normal(0, 1); coordinates=:inference)) # log(J) prior
estimation = EstimationProblem((parameter,), (experiment,))
result = fit(estimation) # load Optim; MAP by default

Here problem is a StationaryPBMProblem with a parameter p.J; load Distributions for Normal. A stationary data vector contains outputs at one operating point. Matrix columns are repeat measurements of that same state. Different feeds, residence times or frozen conditions need separate experiments; kinetic parameters can be shared, while calibration/noise parameters can be experiment-specific. Time-labelled measurements are rejected for this overload.

predictions, likelihood/prior evaluations, MAP, profiles, Gauss–Newton and Laplace approximations, posterior prediction and optional NUTS all use this same statistical model. Failed equilibria become visible NumericalFailures, never valid predictions. The legacy Observation(data, observable) and CalibrationProblem interface also supports stationary calibration; its frequentist likelihood/discrepancy interpretation remains distinct from priors.

For a smooth isolated equilibrium $R(u,\theta)=0$, sensitivities solve

\[\frac{\partial R}{\partial u}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\theta} =-\frac{\partial R}{\partial\theta}.\]

The default ForwardDiff path differentiates these equations, including parameter transforms, bulk coupling, constraints and the observation response. Nested derivatives retain the full posterior Hessian; differentiating Newton's iteration history is unnecessary. The implementation currently forms dense Jacobians and linear factorizations. StationarySolver(condition_limit=...) checks the equilibrated state Jacobian without jitter or a pseudoinverse. Singular roots and unresolved conditioning reject implicit sensitivities. Constraints must remove genuine inventory nullspaces, as in the forward problem.

An eigen-based moment inversion may not support dual numbers. Explicit derivatives=:finite_difference fits the same posterior by resolving each perturbed equilibrium; verify gradient and solve-tolerance refinement. There is no silent derivative fallback. Root branch changes, rank changes and extinction events are not smooth sensitivity paths. A numerical guess selects a branch; it is not a physical initial condition or evidence of uniqueness. The solver restarts from the declared guess at each evaluation, without hidden continuation.

Run the small synthetic MSMPR calibration in estimatestationarycrystallizer.jl:

julia -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.activate("/tmp/pbm-steady"); Pkg.develop(path=pwd()); Pkg.add(["NonlinearSolve", "Optim", "Distributions"])'
julia --project=/tmp/pbm-steady examples/estimate_stationary_crystallizer.jl
# AdvancedHMC and MCMCDiagnosticTools additionally enable --sample.

The example compares exact stationary moments with a joint growth/nucleation fit, verifies gradients and reports local sensitivity-based uncertainty. It uses synthetic measurements and imposes no experimental interpretation.

Continue an operating point

Continuation repeatedly solves the same stationary equations with new physical parameters. It can help reach a difficult operating point or follow a branch:

path = continue_stationary(problem,
    lambda -> merge(problem.p, (feed=3.5+1.5lambda,)), (0.0,1.0))
path.converged || error("requested endpoint was not reached")
last_solution = path.solutions[end]

The parameter callback returns the complete parameter object. The helper solves the anchor, then uses each accepted state as the next guess. It halves failed increments and grows successful ones by a factor of 1.5, within the selected limits. path.attempts retains rejected corrections; partial paths are returned with converged=false. Pure callbacks are required so rejected evaluations do not mutate user state. This is natural-parameter continuation: it cannot follow a fold that reverses the parameter. Pseudo-arclength, automatic mesh transfer, stability analysis and enumeration of all roots are not implemented.

Supported operators and verification

OperatorStationary support
Grid1D sectional residualsGrowth, sources, removal, diffusion and collision residuals with their existing restrictions
Moment residualsExisting weak forms and closure acceptance; no new realizability guarantee for Newton iterates
Grid2D residuals, standalone or in PBMSystemUpwind/WENO5 growth, eligible interior sources/removal, diagonal Ito SizeDiffusion and shared bulk balances; closed/paired-periodic diffusion boundaries. No tensor escape ledger or supplied collision/transfer algorithm.
Axial residuals and coupled axial systemsExisting physical fluxes and local balances; no new boundary interpretation
HOC, characteristic and other complete direct updatesRejected: these do not define a spatial residual
Moment extinction/escape eventsRejected: these require native MeasureEuler events

The analytical MSMPR verification independently checks the finite-volume bidiagonal system, grid convergence against exact bin integrals, and exact unbounded moment equations. A concentration-dependent-growth version checks the coupled material balance against a scalar analytical reduction. Additional tests cover axial feed inventories, diffusion equilibrium, loss rates, conservation constraints and continuation failure. A small residual is a statement about the discretized equations; check size/domain refinement separately. It does not establish uniqueness or dynamical stability. Estimation additionally requires a compatible observation model and a resolved, consistent solution branch.
