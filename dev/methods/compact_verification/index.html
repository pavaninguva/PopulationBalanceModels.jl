Compact-method verification

A compact stencil obtains a derivative or a new time level by solving a banded linear system. This can give high accuracy with few neighboring points, but it does not by itself guarantee stable boundaries, positivity, or the advertised order for variable coefficients. We check those properties separately.

Conservative compact growth

The new HOCFiniteVolume() policy advances bin-integrated populations in one or two size coordinates. It is a conservative adaptation, with fourth-order interior spatial reconstruction and second-order time integration. It supports declared positive size-dependent or prescribed time-dependent growth and physical inflow/outflow. The older HOCADI policy below remains available for periodic constant growth, including its verified fourth-order time composition.

Start with a complete calculation

using PopulationBalanceModels
grid = Grid1D(range(0, 2; length=81); coordinate=:length)
growth = AffineGrowth(0.4, 0.15)
model = PBMModel(growth=Growth(growth))
bc = SizeBC(InflowFlux(1.0), OpenBoundary())
operator = compile(model, grid; evolution=HOCFiniteVolume(), bc)
# Integral of the positive stationary background 1/G(x).
initial = diff(log.(0.4 .+ 0.15 .* grid.edges)) ./ 0.15
problem = PBMProblem(operator, initial, (0.0, 0.4))
solution = simulate(problem; dt=0.0002)
diagnostics(solution).number_accounting[end]

For two coordinates, use Growth2D(SeparableGrowth2D(AffineGrowth(0.4,0.1), ExponentialGrowth(0.3,0.12))) and SizeBC2D(x=bc_x,y=bc_y). Axis lengths, bin counts and spacings may differ. Runnable examples include smooth translations, incoming particles, variable growth and boundary nucleation. The benchmark driver keeps reporting and timing outside those short model definitions. Save both Julia files in the same directory if downloading them separately.

Equation, states and compact faces

The supported physical equation is

\[\frac{\partial f(x,t)}{\partial t} +\frac{\partial [G(x,t)f(x,t)]}{\partial x}=S(x,t),\]

or its separable two-coordinate counterpart,

\[\frac{\partial f(x,y,t)}{\partial t} +\frac{\partial [G_1(x,t)f(x,y,t)]}{\partial x} +\frac{\partial [G_2(y,t)f(x,y,t)]}{\partial y}=S(x,y,t).\]

States remain $N_i=\int_{x_{i-1/2}}^{x_{i+1/2}} f\,dx$ and $N_{ij}=\int\!\int_{C_{ij}} f\,dx\,dy$ per process volume. Thus $U_i=N_i/h$ is a cell average. The independently derived face relation is

\[\frac14\widehat f_{j-1}+\widehat f_j+\frac14\widehat f_{j+1} =\frac34(U_j+U_{j+1}).\]

Here face $j$ lies between cells $j$ and $j+1$. Expanding the cell integrals about that face gives the same terms through $h^2$ on both sides; their difference is $h^4 f^{(4)}/120+O(h^6)$. Consequently the smooth face error is $-h^4 f^{(4)}/180+O(h^6)$ and its interior flux difference is fourth order. This is not an assumption that cell averages are nodal values. For constant periodic growth, eliminating these faces gives $M\dot{\mathbf N}=-gD\mathbf N$, with diagonal/off-diagonal mass weights $1,1/4$ and $D=3(E_+-E_-)/(4h)$. This is exactly the normalized $\Delta t\to0$ spatial limit of Zhang2025 Eq. 15. Uniform bin integration commutes with that constant-coefficient stencil. The new midpoint update does not retain the paper's $(g\Delta t/h)^2$ corrections or claim its fourth-order time formula. For variable growth, multiply the reconstructed density by the face rate: $F_j=G(x_{j+1/2},t)\widehat f_j$. Applying a nodal stencil to $G_iU_i$ would define a different discretization.

Periodic faces wrap. A physical left boundary prescribes number flux directly: OpenBoundary and NoFlux give zero incoming flux; InflowFlux gives the specified flux. Positive growth requires right OpenBoundary. Its trace is the degree-four polynomial reconstruction from the last five cell averages:

\[\widehat f_n=\frac{137}{60}U_n-\frac{163}{60}U_{n-1} +\frac{137}{60}U_{n-2}-\frac{21}{20}U_{n-3}+\frac15U_{n-4}.\]

This outflow trace has fifth-order truncation error. Smooth whole-solve physical-boundary convergence is measured separately below; periodic Fourier stability alone is not a proof for this boundary closure. In 2D each line contains populations integrated over its transverse bin. Incoming InflowFlux values are uniform face-flux densities per transverse size, multiplied by that bin's width. Nonuniform face profiles are not supported.

Complete time update and number balance

At each directional step, freeze prescribed coefficients at the full step's midpoint. Solve for a midpoint number flux using

\[N_i^{\mathrm{new}}=N_i^{\mathrm{old}}+\Delta t(F_{i-1}-F_i),\qquad \mathbf N^{\mathrm{mid}}=\mathbf N^{\mathrm{old}} -\frac{\Delta t}{2}D\mathbf F^{\mathrm{mid}}.\]

Writing the compact relation as $P\widehat{\mathbf f}=Q\mathbf N/h$ gives

\[\left[P\,\operatorname{diag}(1/G^{\mathrm{mid}}) +\frac{\Delta t}{2h}QD\right]\mathbf F^{\mathrm{mid}} =Q\mathbf N^{\mathrm{old}}/h,\]

with known inflow terms moved to the right. $D\mathbf F$ means right minus left flux. The interior system is tridiagonal. Periodic corners and the one-sided outflow row use bounded-rank corrections to a tridiagonal LU: setup, storage and each line solve cost $O(n)$. Factors are reused only when the directional step and all face rates are unchanged. Caches are local to a simulation; remake rechecks parameters and builds fresh factors.

In 2D the composition is half x, full y, half x. Homogeneous separable directional operators commute; the symmetric composition also handles the supported inhomogeneous boundary data to second order. Interior point sources use source/transport/source splitting, with midpoint quadrature at the quarter and three-quarter times. The complete method is second order in time, including prescribed time dependence and this source splitting. No negative substep is used. No transformation $u=Gf$ is used, so time dependence does not lose a $G_t$ contribution. The order statement requires smooth solutions and sufficiently smooth coefficient, source and incoming-flux histories. A discontinuity or singular birth front does not satisfy the smooth spatial-error assumptions.

Nucleation(J,lower_edge) on a physical 1D domain supplies one incoming flux $J(t)$ at the step midpoint. Interior point births contribute their cell integral. A Nucleation2D corner contributes once to the containing cell, not independently through two faces. That singular-source deposition preserves injected number; it does not have smooth-density fourth-order accuracy or an exact subcell size moment. Positive-growth outflow-face births are rejected. Use PrescribedRate((p,t,ctx)->...) for prescribed source/inflow callbacks. Do not specify the same boundary birth again as InflowFlux.

Every actual substep accumulates incoming number, births and the computed signed outflow. At saved times, diagnostics(solution).number_accounting holds inflow and outflow tuples by axis, births, inventory, and

\[\mathrm{defect}=\sum N(t)-\sum N(0) -\mathrm{inflow}-\mathrm{births}+\mathrm{outflow}.\]

These are integrated during the solves, not reconstructed from output samples. They are historical number accounting, not an escaped-particle reservoir with ongoing growth. Sectional state lengths are unchanged. Material moments generally change under growth. Existing moment observations retain their center-weighted bin convention; their quadrature error is separate from the transport order. No population–bulk exchange is implemented for this policy.

Compilation checks the assembled model, not just the growth term. Ordinary unclassified callbacks are rejected, even if diagnostic samples match an affine law. Existing callbacks continue to work for residual methods. For a general law, explicitly declare its assumptions:

law = HOCGrowthLaw((x,p,t,ctx) -> 0.4 + 0.1sin(2pi*x);
    lower_bound=0.3, dependencies=(:coordinate,), periodic=true)

This declares a positive C⁶ law on the entire compiled axis and, here, a smooth periodic extension. It is a user contract, not a mathematical proof obtained from probes. Allowed dependencies are the own :coordinate, :time, :parameters, and prescribed :fields. :bulk, population feedback and the other direction's coordinate are rejected. The lower-bound callback has signature (p,t,ctx) and must bound the full spatial domain. All numerical face values and the bound are checked when constructing/remaking a problem, at coefficient evaluations and at accepted endpoints. The contract must also hold between sampled times; arbitrary callbacks cannot be certified there. Simulation entry repeats the numerical checks, including requests that save only the initial time, so changed parameter containers or fields cannot reuse an earlier problem-construction check. Nonconstant affine/exponential laws require physical boundaries. Unknown periodic laws need periodic=true, including matching derivatives. For this policy, TimeScaledGrowth uses an affine or exponential autonomous spatial law; other prescribed smooth laws use HOCGrowthLaw directly.

For physical boundaries or spatially variable laws, the verified envelope requires $\max(G^{\mathrm{mid}})\Delta t/h\le1$ on each axis, using the numerical face rates. The solver rejects violations and suggests a smaller step. This restriction is not a positivity theorem. Spatially constant periodic growth has unit-modulus midpoint factors for any Courant number, but accuracy still requires time refinement. Resolve narrow source pulses independently of this transport restriction. Dispersive negative lobes remain possible; check_nonnegative=false explicitly retains them for diagnostics without clipping. Even a positive cell state can have a signed reconstructed outflow trace, so outflow accounting uses the actual signed flux.

Relation to the three papers

All three local full papers and the available two-page Li supplement were reviewed. No other supporting information for these papers was present locally.

Primary sourcePublished construction and implementation consequence
Zhang et al. (2025), Eqs. 15, 20, 30–32Nodal density, or autonomous transformed nodal $u=Gf$; two-level three-point HOCD. Eq. 32 is a Godunov point-source addition and does not establish high-order source integration. Its variable-growth coefficients also appear in Li SI S11.
Li et al. (2026), Eqs. 6–16, 18–22; SI S1–S11Nodal 2D ADI with intermediate states and a multilevel recovery, $f^{k+1}=2\bar f^k-f^{k-1}$. Autonomous separable transformation $u=G_1G_2f$; point source added once. This is not a product of the SI one-step updates.
Fu et al. (2025), Eqs. 5, 19–35, 60–84Cell-average characteristic finite volume, conservative parabolic remapping and a compact diffusion solve; second-order time, fourth-order spatial claims. In the linear pure-growth, zero-diffusion limit, the residual characteristic flux vanishes and Eq. 35 reduces to $hU_i^{n+1}=\int_{\bar C_i}f^n dx$. Its compact diffusion operator cancels; it is not an implicit compact growth operator.

The new face interpolation, physical outflow closure and midpoint flux solve above are independently derived, informed by compact reconstruction and Fu's integral conservation requirement. They do not reproduce the Zhang/Li fully discrete coefficients or claim Fu's characteristic remapping algorithm. No artificial diffusion is added to the pure-growth equation.

The full Li formulation does not remove the SI audit's one-step finding: those are different algorithms. In particular, the main Eq. 8 writes an implicit intermediate update from $f^k$, while Eq. 16 recovers from $f^{k-1}$; the correspondence of these stages to SI S11, the startup values and explicit one-sided boundary coefficients are not fully specified. Eqs. 6, 8–15 also print a common $g_1g_2$ multiplier where the physical directions have distinct velocities. We therefore cannot infer an unequal-velocity production scheme by treating those lines as independent S11 steps. The literal and independently corrected nodal one-step audits below remain intact. Their order loss is not presented as a numerical result for the complete published multilevel ADI.

There is also a temporal-stage consistency issue under the literal reading of Eqs. 8 and 16. For an exact advection mode $f^k=e^{-\mathrm i\omega t_k}$, Eq. 16 requires $\bar f^k=\cos(\omega\tau)f^k$. Inserting that value into Eq. 8's apparent intermediate equation $(\bar f^k-f^k)/\tau+K\bar f^k=0$, even with exact $K=\mathrm i\omega$, leaves a residual tending to $\mathrm i\omega f^k$ rather than zero. mainstagecheck.csv records this check; its normalized residual approaches one. This identifies which interpretation needs clarification; it is not a claim that a reconstructed full-paper implementation has been tested and disproved. main_stage_check() in the existing supplement audit reproduces it separately.

Executed refinement and work–precision evidence

The spatial panels use analytic bin integrals, with a fixed small time step across meshes. The temporal panel holds the mesh fixed and compares with an independent dense matrix exponential of the semidiscrete compact operator; this removes spatial error from the time-order measurement. It includes prescribed $G(t)=0.4(1+t^2)$ and a rectangular 2D case. The work–precision panel measures one warmed solve and compares total integrated absolute error, $\sum_i|N_i-N_i^{\mathrm{ref}}|$, at the same physical final time. WENO3/5 use RK4; Inguva2022 uses its separate CFL=1 restriction. This comparison does not assert an efficiency ranking from equal grid sizes.

The extra temporal_affine_inflow rows use the continuum solution $f(x,t)=\exp(-0.15t)$ with $G(x)=0.4+0.15x$ and its exact incoming flux. The spatial reconstruction is exact for this constant density and affine flux, so its time refinement has no spatial-error floor. Boundary, interior and corner source time tests instead use independent augmented matrix exponentials for the same unmodified semidiscrete equations.

The 2D comparison also includes the older periodic fourth-order time composition. It is eligible here because there are no sources or physical boundaries. The new midpoint method often needs more steps to reach small errors: a fourth-order spatial stencil does not remove second-order time error. These are single measured solves after a separate full warm-up, not statistical timing estimates. Very short timings and their ordering are sensitive to runtime noise. Inguva's eligible whole-cell shifts reach the roundoff floor; their small nonzero slopes have no accuracy interpretation. WENO3's finite-range slopes are measured, not assumed to be its nominal order.

This field has $G_1=0.4+0.1x$ and $G_2=0.3\exp(0.12y)$ on $[0,2]\times[0,3]$, at $t=0.25$. The plotted values are bin-average densities on a 48 by 36 mesh, with a characteristic bin-integral reference. The error panel exposes the small dispersive redistribution around the peak; the source-free number remains conserved up to the explicitly recorded boundary flux and roundoff. Negative values are retained in the exports.

For $G=0.4$ and $J(t)=1+0.2\sin(2\pi t)$, the oldest newborn particles reach $x=0.2$ at $t=0.5$. The front and its oscillations are expected for this unlimited compact scheme, not evidence of smooth fourth-order accuracy. At 160 bins, the minimum density is about -0.0503 and the negative population is about $8.57\times10^{-4}$. The numerical number balance is near roundoff, while midpoint integration of the true source count has an error of about $4.19\times10^{-9}$: conservation and quadrature accuracy are different checks. The example also supplies a populated-start run that keeps the normal nonnegativity check enabled. rejections.csv retains the default front rejection and unsupported model/configuration checks.

Exact measured values, allocation counts, step counts, bin-moment errors, number-balance defects and signed minima are in results.csv. M0_error compares reference and computed inventories; it is not the flux-balance defect. M1x_error and M1y_error are respectively $|\sum x_i(N_i-N_i^{\mathrm{ref}})|$ and its y analogue, using bin centers. negative_number is $-\sum\min(N_i,0)$; minimum_density divides each bin number by its width/area before taking the minimum. RMSE is the spatially weighted root-mean-square difference of bin-average densities. orders.csv reports measured slopes rather than nominal labels. environment.txt records CPU, Julia and thread settings. Sharp-front rows retain undershoots; they do not support smooth convergence-rate claims. Standard tests also cover zero-start boundary births, one-time 2D corner deposition, coefficient changes, invalid contracts, dense directional solves and singular systems.

All 80 timed configurations explicitly retain signed values with check_nonnegative=false; completion is not a claim of nonnegative density. The source matrix-exponential tests can converge in time to a signed semidiscrete state. That checks the time update, not the physical accuracy of an unresolved singular distribution. The ordinary example runs retain the default checks, except for the clearly labelled front/field diagnostics.

Earlier periodic compact policy and nodal audits

The public HOCADI method is the periodic constant-velocity compact/Cayley variant specified on the time-integration page. Its Fourier factors have unit modulus. The existing dense-matrix comparisons and long-period tests check that implementation. Negative lobes near a sharp front remain possible. It does not implement the size-dependent algorithm in Li et al. (2026).

Check the supplement independently

examples/verification/li2026_supplement.jl transcribes the five coefficients of the supplement's Eq. S11. For one size coordinate the physical equation is

\[\frac{\partial f(a,t)}{\partial t} +\frac{\partial [G(a)f(a,t)]}{\partial a}=0.\]

For positive, autonomous growth, defining $u(a,t)=G(a)f(a,t)$ gives

\[\frac{\partial u(a,t)}{\partial t} +G(a)\frac{\partial u(a,t)}{\partial a}=0.\]

The printed S11 step is

\[p_{1,i}u_i^{n+1}+p_{2,i}u_{i+1}^{n+1}+p_{3,i}u_{i-1}^{n+1} =p_{1,i}u_i^n+p_{4,i}u_{i+1}^n+p_{5,i}u_{i-1}^n.\]

The audit treats $\tau$ as the time step and $\lambda=\tau/h$. It uses nodal densities on a uniform mesh. Package Grid1D and Grid2D states are bin integrals, so applying these coefficients directly to those states would introduce a separate representation error. The audit is deliberately separate from compile and simulate.

We test three laws on $0\le a\le2$: $G=0.1$, $G=0.1+0.05a$, and $G=0.1\exp(0.2a)$. The last law exercises both first and second growth derivatives in the printed coefficients. The initial density is $\exp[-((a-0.6)/0.08)^2]$. The exact comparison follows characteristics:

\[\frac{da(t)}{dt}=G(a(t)),\qquad f(a,t)=\frac{G(a_0)}{G(a)}f(a_0,0).\]

The one-dimensional run supplies exact time-dependent Dirichlet endpoints. For two dimensions, separable rates allow $u=G_xG_yf$ and a product of the two S11 directional updates. A second Gaussian factor is centered at 0.7 with width 0.1. Its exact solution is negligible at all four faces over $0\le t\le1$; that diagnostic uses zero Dirichlet faces. This directional product is not a transcription of the main paper's multilevel ADI equations or its physical boundary closure.

Results and unresolved issues

At a maximum Courant number of 0.4, the 50–400-interval results initially look fourth order for all three laws. Extending the one-dimensional test to 3200 intervals changes the conclusion: the variable-growth errors eventually approach second order. For $G=0.1+0.05a$ at a maximum Courant number of 0.8, the last two refinement slopes are approximately 2.03 and 2.00. The exponential law gives approximately 2.04 and 1.99. The constant-growth test continues to show fourth-order behavior before roundoff dominates.

The maximum Courant number is a requested bound; the actual time step is shortened uniformly to reach the final time. The CSV records the actual step. At a requested bound of 2, this Dirichlet implementation can become singular or amplify errors enormously. For example, with constant growth and an actual Courant number of 2, S11 has $p_1=0$ and $p_3=0$. The interior tridiagonal matrix with both endpoint values prescribed is singular. This is a concrete boundary/step limitation of this transcription, not evidence that every compact or periodic implementation is unstable.

The main paper's multilevel equations, the relationship to S11, and its high-order physical boundary closure therefore still need reconciliation. We do not attribute altered coefficients to the paper or claim a reproduction of its fourth-order space–time method for variable growth. The conservative adaptation above has its own derivation and evidence. The script retains singular failures, nonfinite errors, negative minima, and coarse-grid oscillations in errors.csv.

A separately specified accuracy correction

The audit also contains an independently derived coefficient variant. It keeps the literal S11 result available for comparison. Under the one-step interpretation above, substituting a smooth exact solution into S11 leaves the following leading time-error contribution after division by the time step:

\[\frac{\tau^2}{24}\left[ \big(G^2G''-G(G')^2\big)\frac{\partial u}{\partial a} -G^2G'\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial a^2}\right].\]

This term vanishes for constant growth and generally remains for variable growth. It explains why a constant-growth test did not expose the order loss. Writing $\lambda=\tau/h$, the independent variant changes the printed coefficients by

\[\widetilde p_1=p_1+\frac{G\tau^2G''}{24},\qquad \delta=\frac{G\tau\lambda G'}{48},\]

\[\widetilde p_2=p_2-\delta,\quad \widetilde p_3=p_3+\delta,\quad \widetilde p_4=p_4-\delta,\quad \widetilde p_5=p_5+\delta.\]

These changes cancel that residual; they are not printed coefficients attributed to Li et al. Select variant=:consistent in the audit's run1d or run2d. For affine and exponential growth, the fine one-dimensional slopes return to approximately 4.00, and the two-dimensional product tests also approach fourth order. The values are retained in consistent_variant.csv.

This is evidence for a specified smooth nodal variant over the tested regime. It does not resolve the main paper's multilevel interpretation, the singular large-step Dirichlet case, positivity, or a general physical boundary closure. Neither audit variant is exposed as a production compile method. The public periodic HOCADI behavior remains unchanged.

What the other supplied papers add

The literature review also identifies schemes with distinct implementation requirements:

SourceConsequence for this package
Zhang et al., CADET Part I (2024)Two-stencil third-order WENO and van Leer limiting motivate the new WENO3 and VanLeer growth choices. Local cell geometry is used on nonuniform 1D meshes. These are not a CADET backend.
Ketcheson et al., PyClaw (2012) and the supplied Clawpack paperWave-propagation methods are a separate solver family. The package's conservative face-flux methods do not implement Clawpack's Riemann-solver machinery.
Kumar et al., Powder Technology 189 (2009), 218–229Cell-average and conservative finite-volume collision algorithms differ from the implemented fixed-pivot event deposition. They need their own number/volume and overflow tests before being exposed.
Zhang et al., CADET Part II (2025), CCE 192, 108860Weighted finite-volume and moment-conserving collision methods are further candidates. A shared collision interface does not mean these algorithms are already implemented.
Inguva et al., Biotechnology Advances 84 (2025), 108643Additional nanoparticle physics motivates explicit material closures. CFD and phase-field coupling remain outside the active package scope.

New growth choices are available through TermMethods(growth=...) and tested for signed velocities and number conservation. Their formal orders and boundary fallbacks are described on the growth page; whole-solve convergence is measured in the constant-growth comparison.
