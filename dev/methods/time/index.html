Time integration

Advancing the assembled equations

After spatial/internal-coordinate discretization the state satisfies

\[\frac{d\mathbf u(t)}{dt}=\mathbf R(\mathbf u(t),t;\mathbf p).\]

$\mathbf u$ contains all bin numbers or moment states and any bulk ODE states. Each term method contributes to $\mathbf R$. Time integration is selected by simulate(problem; method=...,dt=...); it is not a property of Growth or Nucleation.

The native residual-based choices are:

IntegratorStagesTime orderSSP property
ForwardEuler()11Baseline forward-Euler step restriction
SSPRK22()22Inherits a forward-Euler stability property under the same bound
SSPRK33() (default)33Inherits that property under the same bound
SSPRK54()54SSP coefficient about 1.508 relative to forward Euler
RK4()44No general SSP guarantee

These methods advance the same compiled residual for sectional, tensor, moment, coupled-bulk, and axial calculations. Their time order describes smooth ODE solutions, not the complete error of a PBM with a nonsmooth distribution or limited spatial reconstruction. In particular, changing Upwind from Euler to RK4 leaves its first-order spatial diffusion.

SSPRK22 is the explicit trapezoidal (Heun) update:

\[\mathbf u^{(1)}=\mathbf u^m+\Delta t\,\mathbf R(\mathbf u^m,t_m),\qquad \mathbf u^{m+1}=\tfrac12\mathbf u^m+ \tfrac12[\mathbf u^{(1)}+\Delta t\,\mathbf R(\mathbf u^{(1)},t_m+\Delta t)].\]

RK4 evaluates the residual at $t_m$, twice at $t_m+\Delta t/2$, and at $t_m+\Delta t$, with weights $1/6,1/3,1/3,1/6$. Intermediate stage states receive the same state checks as SSPRK33.

SSPRK33() uses the following three-stage update:

\[\begin{aligned} \mathbf u^{(1)}&=\mathbf u^m+\Delta t\,\mathbf R(\mathbf u^m,t_m),\\ \mathbf u^{(2)}&=\frac34\mathbf u^m+\frac14\left[ \mathbf u^{(1)}+\Delta t\,\mathbf R(\mathbf u^{(1)},t_m+\Delta t)\right],\\ \mathbf u^{m+1}&=\frac13\mathbf u^m+\frac23\left[ \mathbf u^{(2)}+\Delta t\,\mathbf R(\mathbf u^{(2)},t_m+\Delta t/2)\right]. \end{aligned}\]

All mechanisms, shared concentrations, and prescribed fields use the current stage state/time. Freezing the concentration while advancing the PSD would be a different method. SSP describes inheritance of a forward-Euler stability property under a compatible step restriction; it does not guarantee positivity for an arbitrary spatial reconstruction.

dt is a maximum fixed step, shortened to land on output times. Default state checks reject nonfinite values and concentrations below -negative_tolerance (default $10^{-12}$), at intermediate stages and completed steps. Values are not clipped. suggest_dt estimates an explicit transport/source bound, including outgoing face velocities and removal/collision hazards. It is not a proven positivity bound for WENO or custom residuals. For 2D unsplit transport the sum of both coordinate Courant numbers matters.

Load SciMLBase to use odeproblem(problem) with an external ODE solver. This exports the same residual, including bulk equations. An adaptive or stiff solver still needs a suitable error scale and treatment of nonnegative states. It does not export direct compact or exact-shift updates.

Constant-growth space–time schemes

For constant growth,

\[\frac{\partial f(a,t)}{\partial t} +g\frac{\partial f(a,t)}{\partial a}=0,\]

Lax–Wendroff uses a time-centered flux. For positive $g$, with $C=g\Delta t/\Delta a$ and $\bar f_i=N_i/\Delta a$, it is

\[F_{i+1/2}=\frac g2(\bar f_i+\bar f_{i+1}) -\frac{g^2\Delta t}{2\Delta a}(\bar f_{i+1}-\bar f_i),\qquad N_i^{m+1}=N_i^m+\Delta t(F_{i-1/2}-F_{i+1/2}).\]

Beam–Warming uses an upwind-biased correction:

\[F_{i+1/2}=g\left[\bar f_i+\frac{1-C}{2}(\bar f_i-\bar f_{i-1})\right].\]

The negative-velocity version reverses the upwind stencil. These are complete second-order space–time schemes, not a face reconstruction to pass through another RK method. At $|C|=1$ both reduce to a cell shift for this homogeneous constant-growth problem. Away from that limit, both can create oscillations and negative densities. There is no limiter or clipping.

using PopulationBalanceModels
grid = Grid1D(0.0:0.01:1.0;coordinate=:length)
model = PBMModel(growth=Growth(AffineGrowth(0.1,0.0)))
bc = SizeBC(OpenBoundary(),OpenBoundary())
operator = compile(model,grid;evolution=LaxWendroff(),bc)
N0 = exp.(-((grid.centers.-0.2)./0.06).^2).*grid.widths
solution = simulate(PBMProblem(operator,N0,(0.0,0.2));
    dt=0.1,check_nonnegative=false) # allow and inspect dispersive undershoots
minimum(solution.u[end])
-1.0587911840678764e-23

Compilation requires a uniform 1D grid with at least three bins, one Growth term with an AffineGrowth(g,0) declaration, no residual-method overrides or prescribed fields, and Periodic, OpenBoundary, or NoFlux size boundaries. Sources, nucleation, bulk feedback, variable growth, and axial transport are rejected. Parameters named in the growth declaration are rechecked when simulating. This implementation restricts both schemes to $|g|dt/\Delta a\le1$; it does not use Beam–Warming's larger possible stability interval.

At an open boundary the prescribed incoming density is zero and outgoing flux uses the adjacent bin. NoFlux sets the boundary face flux to zero; it can retain particles in the last bin. Beam–Warming falls back to upwind at the first interior face missing an upstream cell. These boundary treatments can reduce the full solution's order. dt may shorten at output times; a shortened step loses the exact-shift property unless it also travels a whole cell.

The growth-method comparison records errors, negative number, and elapsed time rather than treating a finished run as evidence of stability or positivity.

Compact/ADI variant

HOCADI(time_order=2) or HOCADI(time_order=4) advances homogeneous constant growth on uniform periodic 2D grids. It rejects sources, bulk coupling, and physical nonperiodic compact boundaries. Its spatial derivative is defined by

\[\frac14d_{i-1}+d_i+\frac14d_{i+1} =\frac{3}{4h}(f_{i+1}-f_{i-1}),\qquad M\mathbf d=D\mathbf f.\]

Each coordinate uses a Cayley solve,

\[\left(M+\frac{g\Delta t}{2}D\right)\mathbf f^{\mathrm{new}} =\left(M-\frac{g\Delta t}{2}D\right)\mathbf f^{\mathrm{old}}.\]

One x solve and one y solve define $S_2$. The fourth-order time composition is

\[S_4(\Delta t)=S_2(\gamma\Delta t)S_2((1-2\gamma)\Delta t)S_2(\gamma\Delta t), \qquad \gamma=(2-2^{1/3})^{-1}.\]

The negative middle step is restricted here to reversible autonomous transport. It must not be applied to irreversible nucleation, collision, or bulk processes. Cyclic tridiagonal factorization uses a rank-two correction; each directional sweep costs linear work in the number of tensor cells.

This is a specified variant, not a verified transcription of Li et al., doi:10.1016/j.partic.2026.01.024. The supplement is now available. Its one-dimensional coefficients can be checked independently, as described in Compact-method verification. Those coefficients define a different update from this periodic variant. The main paper's multilevel update and physical boundary closure still need reconciliation.

Stability is different from positivity

For a periodic Fourier mode with wave angle $\theta$, the compact matrices have symbols $m=1+\frac12\cos\theta$ and $d=\frac{3\mathrm i}{2h}\sin\theta$. A directional Cayley step has amplification

\[A(\theta)=\frac{m-\mathrm i z}{m+\mathrm i z},\qquad z=\frac{3g\Delta t}{4h}\sin\theta,\qquad |A(\theta)|=1.\]

The fourth-order composition is also unit modulus, including its negative step. Thus this periodic constant-growth discretization has no growing Fourier mode in exact arithmetic. It is dispersive and does not preserve positivity. Narrow Gaussians and fronts can develop substantial negative undershoots even while the discrete L2 norm remains constant.

examples/audit_compact_adi.jl checks every resolved Fourier mode over signed Courant numbers 0.1–10 and evolves smooth/rectangular profiles for ten periods. The recorded norm changes are below $5\times10^{-14}$; negative densities remain. Independent dense-matrix tests verify the cyclic solves for both time orders. These results support stability of the implemented periodic variant, not a positivity claim or physical-boundary/paper-fidelity claim. The two-coordinate growth case is a separate, coordinate-dependent physical problem.

Exact integer shifts

ExactShift() implements the eligible constant-growth class of Inguva and Braatz (2023). Each direction must travel an integer number of cells at every step and output time. Periodic or paired open zero-inflow boundaries are supported. Fractional remapping is rejected. An integer shift moves bin numbers exactly; no interpolation or flux reconstruction is required.

ExactShift remains the older constant-velocity interface. For autonomous affine growth, select Inguva2022 or InguvaBraatz2023 during compilation. These trajectory methods construct and check the corresponding physical mesh and time-step restrictions. They do not expose a semi-discrete residual for SSPRK33 or an external ODE solver.

A first-order admissible measure policy

MeasureEuler() is a complete native positive-measure update for the supported raw-moment/sectional systems. It is not another Runge–Kutta tableau. It constructs admissible source/growth/collision substeps, includes optional cumulative losses in those substeps, and couples bulk exchange through their material changes. Its formal order is one, including splitting and bulk coupling. The method contract gives the compatibility matrix, depletion/CFL restrictions and diagnostics. External adaptive ODE solvers do not inherit this guarantee or its atomic boundary events.
