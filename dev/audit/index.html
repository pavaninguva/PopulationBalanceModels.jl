A13: inversion, family feasibility and evolution

Reproduction settings, offending stages/states and numerical measures are in qamar_diagnosis.csv and the independent adaptive calculation in qamar_adaptive.csv. Run test/audit_qamar_diagnosis.jl; --adaptive additionally needs the optional SciMLBase/Tsit5 environment with SpecialFunctions declared directly. The test-only tracer delegates to the actual native SSPRK33 stages and guards. Its private instrumentation is not an extension API. primary_nodes is the requested Gamma count (zero for lognormal), states is the evolved state count, and native precision is the 53-bit state arithmetic with automatic 256-bit Gamma inversion retries. Reference rows identify their 256/512-bit calculations. Native margins are normalized Hankel eigenvalues; reference margins are scaled LDL pivots. Native condition numbers use normalized raw moments; reference condition numbers use deconvolved primary moments. They are distinct necessary diagnostics, not interchangeable error estimates.

For growth and zero-size births, the reference integrates the translated initial distribution on [0,2] and the complete source history:

\[M_k(t)=\int_0^2(x+t)^k n_0(x)\,dx+ \int_0^t\left[100+10^6 e^{-10^4(s-0.215)^2}\right](t-s)^k\,ds.\]

test/fixtures/qamar_reference.jl uses analytic truncated-Gaussian recurrences, with BigFloat inputs and arithmetic at 256 and 512 bits through the critical width calculation. This independently resolves the narrow source pulse and every required order 0–6. Conversion to Float64 occurs only for the final small Jacobi eigenproblem and for testing the package's public Float64 reconstruction.

At 0.4788 s a positive three-kernel reconstruction exists: the normalized primary Hankel condition number is about 1.65e11, a tail weight is about 4.43e-25 of total number, and the independent reconstruction error is below 3e-15. The earlier Float64 boundary solve could reject such a state. Failed Float64 primary inversions now retry the same moment vector and Gamma family with a 256-bit boundary solve/factorization; all moments still have to reconstruct at the requested tolerance. quadrature(...,EQMOM(...)) reports inversion_precision. No ridge, clipping or supplementary particles are used. The native growth/source calculation then first rejects at approximately 0.4790 s; its last saved output remains 0.475 s.

There is also independent feasibility evidence beyond that point. Gamma primary moments satisfy

\[a_k(\sigma)=\sum_{j=1}^k S(k,j)(-\sigma)^{k-j}M_j, \qquad 0\leq\sigma\leq\frac{M_2-M_1^2/M_0}{M_1}.\]

Here S(k,j) are Stirling numbers of the second kind. A nonnegative measure with at most three primary atoms requires a singular 4-by-4 Hankel matrix and positive-semidefinite unshifted/shifted Hankel matrices. The independent sixth-degree determinant polynomial has only one root in the entire allowed width interval at 0.48, 0.49 and 0.50 s. At that root the scaled shifted LDL margin is respectively about -5.14e-5, -4.59e-4, -7.93e-4. Thus none gives a positive three-kernel Gamma representation of these reference moments. Bernstein root isolation agrees at 256/512 bits; this is numerical feasibility evidence using necessary mathematical conditions, not an interval-arithmetic proof for all possible inputs. It is not loss of realizability of the physical characteristic solution.

For the lognormal collision calculation, let v be log-variance and m mean size. The size-sixth-power hazard and uniform-volume daughters give exactly

\[\left.\frac{dM_k}{dt}\right|_{\rm breakage} =\left(\frac{6}{k+3}-1\right)M_{k+6},\qquad M_j=M_0m^j e^{j(j-1)v/2}.\]

The 64/128-point package rates agree with these analytic M6–M8 rates through v=1; a 32-point rule misses M8 there by about 1.15e-3 despite resolving tracked M2. Reconstructing tracked moments does not certify every weak-form integral. Independent adaptive integration, using either quadrature or exact breakage moments, gives the same rapid increase in v. Reparametrizing the analytic closure equations by increasing v reaches v=8 at approximately 0.090468223635 s; tolerance refinement from 1e-11 to 1e-12 changes that time by under 1e-13 s. Number and mean remain finite while inferred higher moments grow. In particular the breakage contribution

\[\frac{dv}{dt}=m^6e^{15v}\left(1-e^{6v}+0.2e^{12v}\right)\]

is positive on its high-width branch and grows rapidly. This is evidence of closure-induced runaway/singular evolution, rather than a defective daughter integral or a remedy obtainable just by halving the explicit step. The runaway precedes the Gaussian source maximum at 0.215 s: the constant background birth flux already broadens the inferred shape. The native 0.0908 s overflow is not an accurate physical singularity time. The comparison retains the failed trajectory and does not call reconstructed M3 conserved.

The collision EQMOM diagnosis remains open at a more specific level. At dt=2e-4, the first failed SSPRK33 predictor is at 0.4459 s with scaled Hankel eigenvalue about -1.21e-7. At dt=1e-4 it fails at 0.44575 s; at 5e-5 it reaches 0.4595 s but then fails primary reconstruction. The 12/48 secondary rules give essentially the same outcomes. A separate RK4 controller using step doubling, fresh trial storage, all stage/acceptance checks and rejection without mutation stalls earlier, around 0.162–0.169 s, depending on tolerance. Tightening primary rtol to 1e-11 does not remove the problem. Its last admissible states pass necessary physical Hankel checks; very small changes in high moments produce large changes in tail nodes and the M12 breakage rate. Thus the retained coarse trajectory is not a time-converged solution of this closure system. A separately specified three-Gamma mixture gives analytic breakage rates through M12; the 12/48-point package rules match them to about 1.02e-13 relative error. This excludes a basic daughter-integral or polynomial secondary-quadrature error in that well-conditioned RHS test, but does not certify evolving ill-conditioned states. Primary inversion/representation conditioning and integration remain unresolved together. Neither the late coarse-step Hankel failure nor the earlier controlled stop proves an intrinsic Gamma-family limit for the physical collision solution. A higher-precision state evolution and independently resolved closure-RHS continuation would be needed to settle that distinction. No collision completion is claimed.
