Observations and parameter estimation

A forward solution stores particle numbers or moments and bulk states. A sensor usually reports a different quantity: a windowed histogram, a weighted size average, a dissolved concentration, or a moment. Define that measurement map before assigning kinetic parameters.

MomentObservable(k) measures a standalone population; for a 2D distribution use a tuple (kx,ky). PopulationMoment(:particles,k) and BulkObservable(:c) measure coupled states. HistogramObservable(edges) integrates a piecewise constant density over experimental bins, which must lie inside the model domain. PopulationHistogram(:particles,edges; weight=x->1, normalize=false) additionally weights pivot numbers and can normalize over the measured window. Normalizing an empty measured distribution raises a numerical failure. An initially empty population is allowed if it is nonempty at the observation time.

FunctionObservable((N,op,p,t)->...) and SystemObservable((views,op,p,t)->...) describe custom scalar or vector outputs. Finite moments do not uniquely specify a histogram: moment populations need an explicit reconstruction/measurement model. Use a FunctionObservable for 2D bins or marginals. An intensity weighting and an instrument's reported average must be defined separately; the CEJ example explicitly uses $M_7/M_6$.

Experiments and likelihood

Observation(times,data,observable; sigma=...) uses rows for outputs and columns for times; a scalar observable accepts a data vector. Times must increase and lie inside the problem interval. Standard deviations are positive, finite, known independent Gaussian errors, supplied as a scalar or matching matrix. Select missing observations before construction. Correlated or normalized histogram errors require a custom likelihood; normalization creates dependence.

An Experiment(problem, observations...) binds operating conditions and initial states to measurements. A CalibrationProblem(experiments; parameters=(z,experiment)->physical_parameters, dt) fits common or experiment-specific parameters through the same forward model. Transform a positive rate as exp(z[1]) and keep its optimization coordinate distinct from the physical rate.

residuals(cal,z) concatenates $(prediction-data)/sigma$ after solving at all measurement times. objective(cal,z) is the corresponding known-variance Gaussian negative log likelihood, including its normalization constant. on_failure=:infinity optionally maps NumericalFailure to infinity; programming or invalid-model errors still propagate. Inspect failure behavior instead of masking errors with arbitrary penalties.

Fitting and interpretation

Load Optim in a separate working environment to activate fit(cal,z0; ...). The adapter returns a FitResult with minimizer, convergence flag, minimum, raw optimizer output, and numerical failure records. Run examples/estimate_kinetic_parameters.jl for an analytical synthetic-data fit.

sensitivity_report(cal,z) finite-differences standardized residuals in optimization coordinates and reports singular values, numerical rank, condition number, and a covariance approximation only at full column rank. This local Gaussian calculation is not a structural identifiability proof or confidence interval. Study step sensitivity, mesh/time error, starting points, and likelihood profiles or resampling before making uncertainty claims. Validated automatic differentiation and adjoints are outside this reference fitting backend.

Load SciMLBase to activate odeproblem(problem; ...); select a compatible solver in the working environment. The adapter exposes the compiled residual, including bulk balances, rather than the direct HOCADI or ExactShift updates. Solver choice does not remove closure, boundary, or positivity restrictions.
