Inferring protein crystallization kinetics

A protein solution may lose dissolved material quickly while its crystals continue to grow for hours. Concentration measurements constrain the amount of material transferred to crystals, but do not reveal how that material is divided among them. A few particle-size measurements can therefore change the kinetic parameters inferred from the same concentration history.

Here an unseeded lysozyme batch at 20 °C supplies both kinds of measurement. The model and experimental markers come from Mitchell et al. (2023), Figure 4, using the power-law nucleation and growth combination called Case 2 in that article. The 100 mL conditions are pH 4.5, 5% w/v NaCl and 210 rpm. We do not pool the 1 L experiment with this one: the paper found that transferring the 100 mL kinetics did not reproduce the larger experiment.

Population and solute balances

For crystal length $\ell$ in µm and time in minutes,

\[\frac{\partial n(\ell,t)}{\partial t} +\frac{\partial[G(t)n(\ell,t)]}{\partial\ell}=0, \qquad G(t)n(0,t)=J(t).\]

The initial population is empty, $n(\ell,0)=0$. With constant solution volume,

\[\frac{dc(t)}{dt} =-3\times10^{-18}\rho k_v\int_0^\infty G(t)\ell^2n(\ell,t)\,d\ell, \qquad c(0)=19.9883\ \mathrm{g\,L}^{-1}.\]

Here $n$ counts actual particles per m³ per µm; $10^{-18}$ converts µm³ to m³. The code additionally rescales particle counts as described below. Nuclei enter at zero length and initially carry no mass. The rates are

\[s(t)=\max\!\left(\frac{c(t)}{c_{\mathrm{eq}}}-1,0\right), \qquad J(t)=60A_Js(t)^j,\qquad G(t)=10^{-3}A_Gs(t)^g.\]

The solubility is 2.16 g/L, crystal density 1240 kg/m³ and volume shape factor 0.81. Temperature is fixed, so activation energies are not inferred. Aggregation, breakage, shear-induced nucleation and dissolution are omitted. These assumptions can cause systematic residuals even if optimization converges.

Units and term definitions in code

Table 7 prints $A_J$ in particles m⁻³ s⁻¹ and $A_G$ in nm/min. The script uses µm, minutes, and particle concentration divided by $10^{12}$. The conversions are explicit:

saturation(p,ctx) = max(ctx.bulk.c/p.ceq-1,zero(ctx.bulk.c))
growth(L,p,t,ctx) = 1e-3*p.AG*saturation(p,ctx)^p.g
nucleation(p,t,ctx) = 60/1e12*exp(p.logAJ)*saturation(p,ctx)^p.j
model = PBMModel(
    growth=Growth(rate=growth),
    primary=Nucleation(rate=nucleation,size=0.),
)
operator = compile(model,grid;methods=TermMethods(
    growth=MUSCL(),by_term=(primary=BoundaryInjection(:left),)))
system = PBMSystem((crystals=operator,);nonnegative_bulk=(:c,),
    exchanges=(ConservativeExchange(:crystals,:c,
        (L,p,t,ctx)->1e-6*p.rho*p.kv*L^3),))

The factor $10^{-6}=10^{12}(10^{-6})^3$ converts a scaled particle count times µm³ to material volume per solution volume. The exchange uses the numerical third-moment change, including finite first-bin deposition. Grid refinement checks that this finite-bin effect is small. No numerical seed population is inserted.

Experimental readings and observation model

The supplied CSV contains 28 red concentration markers and four green diameter markers read from the native Figure 4 image. They are digitized experimental readings, not raw laboratory records or synthetic measurements. The extraction script retains pixel centres, axis calibration, PDF hash and a source citation. The zero-time diameter symbol is excluded because a median is undefined for an empty population.

Concentration is observed directly. The script interprets diameter $D_{50}$ as a volume-weighted median and integrates the piecewise-constant number density with weight $\ell^3$:

\[\int_0^{D_{50}(t)}\ell^3n(\ell,t)\,d\ell =\frac12\int_0^\infty\ell^3n(\ell,t)\,d\ell.\]

This observation convention needs confirmation against the original sizing instrument. It is stated as an assumption, not recovered metadata. A median is not $\mu_4/\mu_3$. Nor can it be uniquely determined from four moments without an additional reconstruction model. The example therefore fits a sectional PSD.

concentration = Measurement(data.concentration.t,data.concentration.y,
    BulkObservable(:c);likelihood=GaussianLikelihood(sigma=0.1),
    source=:figure4_concentration)
sizes = Measurement(data.D50.t,data.D50.y,FunctionObservable(volume_median);
    likelihood=GaussianLikelihood(sigma=0.5),source=:figure4_D50)

The article calls 0.1 g/L and 0.5 µm variances despite giving unsquared units. We interpret them as Gaussian standard deviations. The two blocks are assumed independent; laboratory covariance and instrument response are unavailable. Graph-reading error and model inadequacy are additional uncertainties, not eliminated by choosing these noise scales.

Declare priors and estimate parameters

Table 3 lists initial guesses and fitting ranges, not Bayesian priors. In particular, its initial growth-coefficient range 600–1500 nm/min excludes the final reported value 0.367 nm/min. Reusing that range as a hard prior would prevent recovery of the reported fit.

The example makes its own choices: uniform physical densities for $\log A_J\in[0,100]$, $j\in[1,10]$ and $g\in[1,3]$. The first quantity is itself the named physical parameter logAJ. A normal prior on $\log A_G$ is centred on the reported final coefficient, with standard deviation 1.5. That last prior is informed by the published fit to the same data, so this is an illustrative conditional analysis, not independent validation. Change growth_prior_sd and refit to assess its influence.

ParameterSpec(:AG;initial=0.367,transform=LogTransform(),
    prior=Prior(Normal(log(0.367),1.5);coordinates=:inference))
ParameterSpec(:j;initial=2.14,transform=LogitTransform(1,10),
    prior=Prior(Uniform(1,10);coordinates=:physical))

estimation_problem joins the parameter declarations, both measurement blocks and the coupled PBM. fit uses the shared posterior with ForwardDiff gradients; its starting gradient is independently checked. Transform Jacobians are applied once for physical-coordinate priors. The reported physical values are transforms of an inference-coordinate MAP; their mode depends on that coordinate choice.

using PopulationBalanceModels, Optim
include("examples/protein_crystallization.jl")
problem = ProteinCrystallization.estimation_problem(bins=100)
result = fit(problem;maxiters=200)
result.diagnostics

Fit, interpretation and refinement

The plot includes predictions using the printed Table 7 coefficients, this example's MAP fit, and predictions on a doubled size grid with those fitted parameters. Differences from the paper include finite-volume treatment, the stated observation convention, graph-read data and prior assumptions. Agreement with some concentration points is not enough to establish that all four kinetic parameters are identifiable. The sharp concentration decrease and the small number of size observations also leave room for model discrepancy.

The committed 80-bin run converged with an infinity-norm gradient of about 0.008. Its verified Laplace calculation was rejected because the Hessian did not stabilize under step halving. The PSD median and limiter introduce changes of derivative across bin and limiter branches; tighter numerics and profiles are needed before reporting uncertainty. The scripts report this failure openly.

fit.txt records convergence, the gradient norm, likelihood/prior contributions and whether a verified Laplace calculation succeeded. Invalid curvature is reported rather than regularized. No MCMC convergence or reproduction of the paper's confidence intervals is claimed. A doubled-grid prediction check is not a refit: use refinement_study to compare fitted parameters under numerical refinement before treating intervals as reliable.

Run from an environment containing the package and optional Optim dependency:

julia --project=. examples/protein_crystallization.jl
python docs/benchmarks/plot_new_case_studies.py protein --usetex

Optim must be installed in that environment. The CSV is included, so the PDF and Python extraction tools are not needed to run Julia. The optional solver argument accepts a checked SciML solver adapter when adaptive integration is preferred. examples/data/protein_crystallization/README.md gives extraction provenance and the assumptions that still need experimental confirmation.

Complete runnable script

The code below is read from the example source during the documentation build. Run it from the repository root using the environment described above.

examples/protein_crystallization.jl

module ProteinCrystallization
using PopulationBalanceModels
using Distributions: Uniform, Normal
export parameters,build,volume_median,observations,estimation_problem,run_study

# Time is minutes, length µm, c is g/L (=kg/m³); N is in 10^12 particles/m³.
# Table 7 Case 2: AJ has units m^-3 s^-1; AG is printed in nm/min.
parameters()=(logAJ=18.9,j=2.14,AG=.367,g=2.45,ceq=2.16,rho=1240.,kv=.81)
saturation(p,ctx)=max(ctx.bulk.c/p.ceq-1,zero(ctx.bulk.c))
growth(L,p,t,ctx)=1e-3*p.AG*saturation(p,ctx)^p.g
nucleation(p,t,ctx)=60/1e12*exp(p.logAJ)*saturation(p,ctx)^p.j

"""Unseeded isothermal protein crystallization, power-law kinetics (paper Case 2).
Rates are size-independent; no aggregation, breakage, dissolution or shear model.
"""
function build(;bins=100,Lmax=40.,p=parameters(),c0=19.9883,tf=700.,scheme=MUSCL())
    grid=Grid1D(range(0.,Lmax;length=bins+1);coordinate=:length)
    model=PBMModel(growth=Growth(rate=growth),primary=Nucleation(rate=nucleation,size=0.))
    op=compile(model,grid;methods=TermMethods(growth=scheme,by_term=(primary=BoundaryInjection(:left),)))
    system=PBMSystem((crystals=op,);nonnegative_bulk=(:c,),exchanges=(
        ConservativeExchange(:crystals,:c,(L,p,t,ctx)->1e-6*p.rho*p.kv*L^3),))
    PBMProblem(system,(crystals=zeros(bins),),(0.,tf);bulk0=(c=c0,),p)
end

"""Volume-weighted D50 of the declared piecewise-constant sectional density.
Integrate L³ over each bin, then invert its cumulative integral. This is a median,
not a ratio of moments. A raw-moment closure needs a separate reconstruction.
"""
function volume_median(u,op,p,t)
    g=op.system.populations.crystals.grid;N=population(op,u,:crystals)
    mass=N.*(g.edges[2:end].^4-g.edges[1:end-1].^4)./(4 .*g.widths)
    total=sum(mass)
    total>0 || throw(NumericalFailure("D50 is undefined for an empty population"))
    all(>=(-1e-10),mass) || throw(NumericalFailure("invalid negative particle volume"))
    target=total/2;running=zero(total)
    for i in eachindex(mass)
        if running+mass[i]>=target
            fraction=(target-running)/mass[i]
            return (g.edges[i]^4+fraction*(g.edges[i+1]^4-g.edges[i]^4))^(1/4)
        end
        running+=mass[i]
    end
    throw(NumericalFailure("D50 inversion failed"))
end

function observations()
    lines=readlines(joinpath(@__DIR__,"data","protein_crystallization","figure4_observations.csv"))[2:end]
    rows=[split(line,',') for line in lines]
    function selected(name)
        r=filter(x->x[1]==name,rows)
        (;t=[parse(Float64,x[2]) for x in r],y=[parse(Float64,x[3]) for x in r],sigma=parse(Float64,first(r)[4]))
    end
    (;concentration=selected("concentration"),D50=selected("D50"))
end

"""One posterior for graph-read concentration and volume-median observations.
Table 3 supplies starts/bounds, not priors. Here logAJ∈[0,100], j∈[1,10], g∈[1,3]
are explicit uniform-prior assumptions; log(AG) has a weak normal prior centred
on Table 7's Case 2 estimate. Change growth_prior_sd to examine that assumption.
The two measurements have independent Gaussian errors, interpreting the paper's
0.1 g/L and 0.5 µm as standard deviations. Raw instrument covariance is absent.
"""
function estimation_problem(;bins=100,solver=nothing,dt=.5,growth_prior_sd=1.5)
    p=parameters();problem=build(;bins,p);data=observations()
    concentration=Measurement(data.concentration.t,data.concentration.y,BulkObservable(:c);
        likelihood=GaussianLikelihood(sigma=data.concentration.sigma),source=:figure4_concentration)
    sizes=Measurement(data.D50.t,data.D50.y,FunctionObservable(volume_median);
        likelihood=GaussianLikelihood(sigma=data.D50.sigma),source=:figure4_D50)
    specs=(
        ParameterSpec(:logAJ;initial=p.logAJ,prior=Prior(Uniform(0,100);coordinates=:physical)),
        ParameterSpec(:j;initial=p.j,transform=LogitTransform(1,10),prior=Prior(Uniform(1,10);coordinates=:physical)),
        ParameterSpec(:AG;initial=p.AG,transform=LogTransform(),prior=Prior(Normal(log(p.AG),growth_prior_sd);coordinates=:inference)),
        ParameterSpec(:g;initial=p.g,transform=LogitTransform(1,3),prior=Prior(Uniform(1,3);coordinates=:physical)))
    ex=InferenceExperiment(:batch,problem,concentration,sizes;dt=solver===nothing ? dt : nothing,solver)
    EstimationProblem(specs,(ex,))
end

function run_study(;output=joinpath(@__DIR__,"..","docs","src","assets","protein_crystallization"),bins=80,
        solver=nothing,dt=.5,maxiters=200)
    # Optional dependency loaded by the caller, preserving the package extension.
    Base.get_extension(PopulationBalanceModels,:PopulationBalanceModelsOptimExt)!==nothing ||
        error("Load Optim before run_study (see the complete-script invocation).")
    mkpath(output);problem=estimation_problem(;bins,solver,dt)
    fitresult=fit(problem;maxiters,gradient_tolerance=.02)
    theta=physical_parameters(problem,fitresult.minimizer,:batch)
    open(joinpath(output,"fit.txt"),"w") do io
        println(io,"Digitized experimental markers: Mitchell et al. (2023), Figure 4; no raw laboratory data.")
        println(io,"MAP converged: ",fitresult.converged,"; gradient norm: ",fitresult.diagnostics.gradient_norm)
        println(io,"Coordinates: identity(logAJ), logit(j), log(AG), logit(g); theta=",theta)
        println(io,"Posterior: ",posterior_components(problem,fitresult.minimizer))
        println(io,"Volume-weighted D50 is an explicit observation assumption; verify against the original instrument convention.")
    end
    times=collect(0.:5.:700.)
    open(joinpath(output,"predictions.csv"),"w") do io
        println(io,"method,bins,time_min,concentration_g_L,D50_um,material_drift")
        for (label,n,p) in (("reported_parameters",bins,parameters()),("MAP",bins,merge(parameters(),theta)),("MAP_refined",2bins,merge(parameters(),theta)))
            pb=build(bins=n,p=p)
            solution=solver===nothing ? simulate(pb;dt=dt/2,saveat=times) : solver(pb,times)
            grid=pb.operator.system.populations.crystals.grid
            for (t,u) in zip(solution.t,solution.u)
                D50=t==0 ? NaN : volume_median(u,pb.operator,p,t)
                drift=bulk(pb,u,:c)+1e-6*p.rho*p.kv*moment(grid,population(pb,u,:crystals),3)-pb.u0[end]
                println(io,join((label,n,t,bulk(pb,u,:c),D50,drift),','))
            end
        end
    end
    # Weak posterior curvature is reported, never repaired into confidence.
    try
        lap=laplace_approximation(problem,fitresult)
        open(joinpath(output,"fit.txt"),"a") do io;println(io,"Laplace: ",lap);end
    catch e
        e isa LaplaceFailure || rethrow()
        open(joinpath(output,"fit.txt"),"a") do io;println(io,"Laplace unavailable: ",sprint(showerror,e));end
    end
    fitresult
end
if abspath(PROGRAM_FILE)==@__FILE__
    @eval using Optim
    run_study()
end
end
