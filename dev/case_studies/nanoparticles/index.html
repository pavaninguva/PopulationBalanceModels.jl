Lipid nanoparticle formation: CEJ recipe

With D_c defined as a positive loss magnitude, the physical bookkeeping is

\[\partial_t n+\partial_L(Gn) =B_n+B_c-D_c.\]

The recipe implements the following components in diameter/length coordinates:

Mechanism/closureImplementation
Solvent historyPrescribed aqueous fraction w(t); no CFD
SolubilityUser-supplied C_infinity(w,T), with a separate log-linear mole-solubility helper
CNT creationCritical diameter and nucleation rate from Eqs. 7-8
Creation-size spreadGaussian centered at the current critical diameter; width and normalization explicit
Growth/dissolutionSigned G=kg L Cinfinity (S-S_star)
CoalescenceBrownian kernel times a supplied attachment law and fitted PEG factor
Material balanceShared free-lipid state coupled to the actual particle-volume update
DilutionSame volumetric dilution rate applied to particles and dissolved lipid
MeasurementsL^6-weighted normalized histogram and final-paper Eq. 3, M7/M6

The implemented CNT formulas are

\[L_c=\frac{4\gamma V_m}{k_BT\ln S},\qquad B_0=A_n\exp\left[-\frac{16\pi\gamma^3V_m^2} {3(k_BT)^3(\ln S)^2}\right],\]

with B0=0 for S<=1. Vm is PER-MOLECULE volume, consistent with kB. A molar volume would require the matching thermodynamic convention.

The collision kernel is

\[\beta(L,\lambda)= k_{\rm PEG}\alpha_{\rm base}(L,\lambda,w,T) \frac{2k_BT(L+\lambda)^2}{3\mu L\lambda}.\]

The attachmentwithoutpeg callback returns alphabase; it must not already include the PEG factor. Parameters kg, An and kPEG remain available for native fitting through the problem parameter tuple.

The final paper reports An=2.0e22 m^-3 s^-1, kg=11.88 m^3 kg^-1 s^-1 and k_PEG=6.5e-6, and uses an interfacial energy near 0.010 N/m. Reusing those values under a changed source normalization or concentration basis requires verification, not a mechanical substitution.

Diameter-space coalescence

An event produces a particle with

\[L_{\rm product}=(L_1^3+L_2^3)^{1/3}.\]

Use AdditiveCoordinate(L->L^3,cbrt) for fixed-pivot interpolation. This keeps L as the transport/measurement coordinate while conserving M3. Depositing a product at L1+L2 would conserve the wrong physical quantity.

The equivalent number-density transformation to q=L^3 is

\[n_q(q)=n_L(L)/(3L^2).\]

Changing coordinates without the Jacobian changes the physical distribution. For a physical volume v=(pi/6)L^3, the corresponding Jacobian is different.

Mixing and free-lipid balance

For the delayed-mixing case,

\[w(t)=\frac{\mathrm{FRR}}{\mathrm{FRR}+1}(1-e^{-t/\tau}).\]

An explicit material-element interpretation gives V/Vorganic=1/(1-w). The dilution hazard is lambda=w_dot/(1-w). With particle concentrations and free lipid both defined per current mixture volume, the recipe applies

\[\dot c=-\lambda c -\rho_p\frac{\pi}{6} \left(\dot M_3\big|_{\rm growth}+\dot M_3\big|_{\rm nucleation}\right).\]

There is also a -lambda*n term in the PBE. Coalescence does not consume dissolved lipid. Absent truncation/other losses, the conserved material is

\[V(t)\left[c(t)+\rho_p\frac{\pi}{6}M_3(t)\right].\]

This balance is an explicitly declared completion of the model. The main paper discusses lipid consumption but does not print a complete free-lipid ODE.

For tau=0, use the already mixed initial concentration Corganic/(FRR+1). For tau>0, the model starts with Corganic before dilution. The delayed recipe currently begins at t=0; later-start initialization needs a custom operating history/state.

Required inputs and conventions

InputWhy it stays explicit
Initial distribution and diameter limitsThe paper mentions 200 logarithmic bins but does not give a complete grid/initial-state specification
Initial dissolved concentration/basisMust agree with instantaneous versus delayed mixing
Temperature and viscosity lawRequired by CNT/Brownian kernels; process conditions must be confirmed
Molecular volume and particle densityNeeded to translate thermodynamics and particle volume into mass
C_infinity(w,T)Log-linear mole solubility must be converted to the concentration units used for growth
S_star(L,w,T)A size-dependent equilibrium closure is referenced but not fully specified
Nucleation widthThe size-spread width must be distinct from interfacial energy
Gaussian normalizationTotal number rate and unnormalized density amplitude differ dimensionally
Attachment lawResolve the energy/separation/stability-ratio convention against author implementation

A conventional Kelvin-ratio helper is provided as an OPTIONAL closure. It is not automatically asserted to be the author's exact S_star implementation.

The inherited recipe notes describe the two-page supplement specifies DLVO energy expressions, A_H=1.7e-22 J and a selected surface potential of 10 mV. Its screening formula gives an inverse length although kappa is described as a Debye length. Ion concentration units, separation coordinate and electrostatic prefactors need a consistent interpretation. The recipe accepts a confirmed attachment function or table rather than silently repairing the printed implementation.

Additional printed conventions to reconcile:

  • Eq. 1 adds Dc while Eq. 11 defines a positive integral. The recipe uses parent removal, Bc-D_c.
  • Eq. 6 prints a Gaussian without its density-normalization prefactor, and reuses sigma notation later used for interfacial energy.
  • Eq. 14 prints an energy-barrier integral where attachment versus stability ratio, reciprocal and distance conventions need confirmation.
  • Eq. 10's kernel arguments need to agree with the length arguments in Eq. 12.

These points are reproduction questions, not evidence about what the author's actual code computes.

Observations and calibration

For the final-paper definition,

\[n_{\rm intensity}(L)=L^6 n(L),\qquad d_{\rm paper}=M_7/M_6.\]

paperintensitymean uses that exact ratio under an explicit name. The normalized histogram is a separate observable, with its measurement window and weighting specified. Do not equate different instrument averages without checking their definitions.

Joint PSD, free-lipid and number/moment measurements can share fitted kinetic parameters through CalibrationProblem. The article's late-time observations provide limited information about the absolute millisecond kinetic scale. Testing multistart, profile likelihood and numerical refinement is part of the planned inference validation.

Run the model recipe

Run examples/lipid_nanoparticle_demo.jl using the reusable examples/papers/cej2025_lnp.jl. The demonstration starts with free lipid 10 kg/m³, FRR 3, mixing time 0.01 s, and 60 logarithmic diameter bins from 1 nm to 2 micrometers. SSPRK33 uses a 20-microsecond step to 0.02 s. Temperature, viscosity, solubility, equilibrium ratio, attachment, molecular volume, density, and nucleation width are explicitly supplied demonstration choices. The growth constant also differs from the paper's reported value. Overflow is discarded explicitly.

The script reports particle concentration, the defined intensity mean in nm, and free lipid. These outputs demonstrate executable coupling and observation paths. They are not validated predictions or reproductions of published figures. A derivative-level test checks the declared material-element dilution balance; full experimental comparison needs confirmed inputs, conventions, and refinement. Late-time normalized PSDs alone may poorly identify an absolute fast kinetic scale.

Reference: Shin et al. (2025), Chemical Engineering Journal 523, 167786, doi:10.1016/j.cej.2025.167786. The original supplementary source is identified in the recipe comments; its unresolved conventions remain explicit above.

Numerical output

With the stated synthetic closures on Julia 1.10.3, the script returned particle concentration 2.80014e18 m⁻³, intensity mean 3.24097 nm, and free lipid 3.51500 kg/m³. These magnitudes belong to the demonstration recipe only.

Executable source

This listing is read directly from the runnable script during the documentation build.

Source on GitHub

using PopulationBalanceModels
include("papers/cej2025_lnp.jl")
using .CEJ2025LNP

# Wiring demonstration with EXPLICIT SYNTHETIC CLOSURES. These values do not
# establish a reproduction of the CEJ paper. All mechanisms and coupling paths
# are exercised; replace closures/inputs with the author's confirmed model.
grid=Grid1D(exp.(range(log(1e-9),log(2e-6);length=61));coordinate=:diameter)
problem=CEJ2025LNP.build(;grid,initial_lipid=10.0,FRR=3.0,tau=0.01,
    temperature=t->298.15,viscosity=(w,T)->1e-3,
    solubility=(w,T)->10.0*exp(-8w),
    equilibrium_ratio=(L,w,T)->1.0,
    attachment_without_peg=(L,M,w,T)->1.0,
    molecular_volume=5e-28,particle_density=1000.0,nucleation_width=1e-9,
    nucleation_normalization=:total_rate,tspan=(0.0,0.02),kg=0.05,
    overflow=:discard)
solution=simulate(problem;dt=2e-5,saveat=[0.0,0.005,0.02])
N=population(problem,solution.u[end],:lnp)
println("Synthetic demonstration particle concentration: ",sum(N))
println("Paper-defined intensity mean [nm]: ",1e9*paper_intensity_mean(grid,N))
println("Free lipid [kg/m^3]: ",bulk(problem,solution.u[end],:lipid))
