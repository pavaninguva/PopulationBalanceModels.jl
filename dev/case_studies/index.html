Case studies

These calculations show how a physical population balance becomes a working model. Each develops the equations alongside the rate callbacks, initial state, boundaries, and numerical choices used in its implementation.

  • Uncertainty in continuous milling: compare the deterministic startup with Barrasso's Figure 7, then propagate four uncertain breakage/screen parameters by MC and PCE. The partial forward reproduction and the retained UQ reconstruction have distinct configurations and limits.

  • Batch cooling crystallization: seeds grow and new crystals nucleate as cooling changes solubility. The population consumes dissolved solute, so the concentration balance is solved with the distribution.

  • Comparing growth and time methods: a translating distribution separates spatial diffusion, temporal error, and dispersive undershoots; the analytical solution also tests the CFL=1 cell shift.

  • Aggregation and nucleation with moments: an analytical aggregation–fragmentation balance and a short nucleation pulse test QMOM, rank-adaptive QMOM, Gamma EQMOM, DQMOM and lognormal closure, separating shape, reconstruction and time-integration errors.

  • Lipid nanoparticle formation: an initially particle-free solution nucleates, grows, and coalesces after solvent mixing. Scattering observations weight the resulting distribution strongly toward larger particles. Historical full-horizon curves are diagnostic: the current strict stiff driver fails at startup, while a short native startup is checked.

  • Growth in two particle dimensions: coordinate-dependent growth moves and stretches a joint distribution. An analytical solution exposes numerical broadening, oscillations, and errors under grid refinement.

  • Estimating size-dependent growth: analytical density curves with Gaussian noise illustrate fitting two growth rates, comparing prior choices and checking MCMC chains.

  • Continuous tubular precipitation: axial transport and dispersion carry both solute and crystals; startup and outlet distributions are compared under refinement.

  • Calibrating an isothermal protein batch: original concentration and number distributions calibrate CNT and CNT with growth dispersion, followed by frozen predictions of later observations.

  • Stem-cell self-renewal and differentiation: intracellular marker production, binary division and exit from pluripotency reshape a cell population. Synthetic examples distinguish cohort dynamics from distributions sampled at division and differentiation events.

The constant-growth and two-dimensional calculations have analytical solutions. The cooling calculation uses an explicitly chosen nonnegative initial condition and a finer sectional reference, with an independent moment check. The nanoparticle reconstruction retains historical profiles with unresolved full-horizon replay and measurement conventions. These qualifications stay beside the relevant inputs and figures. The axial reactor tutorial adds a physical position coordinate to an intrinsic growth model.

Each page ends with a minimal calculation and links to its full reproduction driver. Use the common reproduction setup to keep recipes, data and plotting helpers together. Figure-specific assumptions, reference qualifications and numerical limits stay beside the results they affect.
