Comparing growth and time methods

A population can broaden for two different reasons: its particles may have different growth rates, or its numerical approximation may smear the size distribution. A constant-growth calculation separates these effects. Every particle moves at the same rate, so the true distribution translates without changing shape. Any broadening, oscillation, or loss of number comes from the calculation or its boundaries.

This model follows Case 1 of Inguva, Schickel, and Braatz (2022). It also demonstrates a special result from that study: at a Courant number of one, some simple schemes reduce to an exact cell shift. A more accurate ODE integrator does not necessarily preserve that cancellation.

Model and analytical solution

For intrinsic coordinate $a\ge0$, solve

\[\frac{\partial f(a,t)}{\partial t} +0.1\frac{\partial f(a,t)}{\partial a}=0, \qquad f(a,0)=100\exp(-a/0.01).\]

There are no births, deaths, or collisions. The left boundary supplies no new particles. The analytical solution before the upper boundary matters is

\[f(a,t)= \begin{cases} 100\exp[-(a-0.1t)/0.01],&a\ge0.1t,\\ 0,&a<0.1t. \end{cases}\]

The runner uses $0\le a\le1$ and stops at $t=4$. It applies a zero-flux upper boundary as in the source comparison. The initial tail near that boundary is exponentially small; the dominant feature travels to $a=0.4$. The sharp leading edge is a difficult test for unlimited high-order schemes.

Specify the law, grid, and boundaries

using PopulationBalanceModels
include(joinpath(pkgdir(PopulationBalanceModels),"examples","constant_growth_methods.jl"))
import .ConstantGrowthMethods
const comparison = ConstantGrowthMethods

model = PBMModel(growth=Growth(AffineGrowth(0.1,0.0)))
grid = Grid1D(range(0.0,1.0;length=201);coordinate=:length)
bc = SizeBC(OpenBoundary(),NoFlux())
N0 = comparison.exact_counts(grid,0.0)

AffineGrowth(0.1,0.0) declares a constant velocity in coordinate units per time. N0 contains bin integrals of the initial density. Sampling the steep initial exponential only at bin centers would introduce an avoidable initial quadrature error.

Pair a face reconstruction with an integrator

operator = compile(model,grid;methods=TermMethods(growth=MUSCL()),bc)
problem = PBMProblem(operator,N0,(0.0,4.0))
dt = 0.5*first(grid.widths)/0.1
solution = simulate(problem;method=SSPRK33(),dt)

Changing MUSCL() to Upwind(), VanLeer(), WENO3(), WENO5(), Centered(), or LinearUpwind() changes the growth face flux. Changing SSPRK33() to ForwardEuler(), SSPRK22(), SSPRK54(), or RK4() changes how the assembled residual advances in time. The runner compares selected combinations, rather than assuming every pairing is stable at the same step size. The growth and time pages describe their stencils, boundaries, and restrictions.

Lax–Wendroff and Beam–Warming include their own time updates:

lw = compile(model,grid;evolution=LaxWendroff(),bc)
lw_problem = PBMProblem(lw,N0,(0.0,4.0))
lw_solution = simulate(lw_problem;dt,check_nonnegative=false)
minimum(lw_solution.u[end])
-0.05855280038454415

evolution=BeamWarming() selects the other complete update. These compiled operators reject variable growth and additional mechanisms. They do not supply an ODE residual for a second integrator. Negative values are deliberately retained in this benchmark and exported; the normal default state checks would reject a run with a substantial undershoot.

Compare the solutions

The upper panels show upwind, limited linear and WENO reconstructions; the lower panels show the unlimited reconstructions and complete space–time methods. All seven supplied 1D face reconstructions appear. Inguva2022 appears at its required matched CFL=1, with paired open boundaries; its tiny outgoing exponential tail is negligible here. The other front runs keep the closed upper boundary stated above.

At $C=0.5$, upwind broadens the peak. Increasing its time order does not remove the spatial diffusion. MUSCL and WENO reduce that broadening, while the unlimited schemes can create appreciable negative lobes. Their negative number and minimum density are reported in errors.csv.

At $C=1$, Upwind with ForwardEuler, Lax–Wendroff, and Beam–Warming shift the bin numbers one cell per step, within floating-point error. Upwind with RK4 still solves the semi-discrete upwind equations and remains diffusive. The exact cancellation is a property of the complete update, not of the upwind spatial stencil alone. The existing Inguva2022() method provides a direct shift on a matched travel-time grid; its variable-growth extension is explained in Transport along growth trajectories.

LinearUpwind needs a smaller step: the runner omits its $C=1$ pairing, which is outside RK4's stability region. A transport CFL of one is not a general stability bound for higher-order stencils.

Lax–Wendroff at $C=0.5$ is stable for periodic constant advection but is not monotone. For a Fourier wave of angle $\theta$, its squared amplification is

\[|A(\theta)|^2=1-4C^2(1-C^2)\sin^4(\theta/2)\le1,\qquad |C|\le1.\]

The tests compare a signed-velocity step against the expanded difference formula and check the discrete squared norm over a full domain transit. The front's negative lobes are dispersive oscillations; calling them instability would obscure the distinction between bounded error and positivity.

Measure error and work

For each run, compare numerical bin averages with analytical bin averages:

\[\bar f_i^{\mathrm{exact}}(t)=\frac{1}{\Delta a_i} \int_{a_{i-1/2}}^{a_{i+1/2}}f(a,t)\,da,\]

\[E_{\mathrm{RMS}}= \left[\frac{1}{a_{\max}-a_{\min}} \sum_i\Delta a_i(\bar f_i-\bar f_i^{\mathrm{exact}})^2\right]^{1/2}.\]

The runner records absolute errors, total-number drift, negative number, step count, and elapsed time. It first warms each calculation, then reports the median and range of three runs. Timings include stepping, residual assembly, and allocations on the generating machine.

The paper's exponential and the zero-inflow front are nonsmooth. Their RMSE curves measure how well a scheme resolves that front; they cannot establish smooth-solution order. A second test uses periodic boundaries and the smooth, positive initial density

\[f(a,0)=1+0.2\sin(2\pi a)+0.1\cos(4\pi a),\qquad 0\le a\le1.\]

Its analytical solution is $f(a,t)=f(a-0.1t,0)$ with periodic continuation. Both initialization and comparison use exact bin integrals. At fixed $C=0.5$, Lax–Wendroff and Beam–Warming approach second-order RMSE convergence. WENO5 with SSPRK3 approaches third order because time error eventually dominates. WENO5 with RK4 reaches much smaller errors. A companion run reduces its time step as $\Delta t\propto h^{5/4}$, so fourth-order time error cannot mask fifth-order spatial convergence. Error cancellation and nonlinear weights can produce slopes above the formal order over a finite refinement range; those slopes do not establish a new order of accuracy.

The MUSCL and van Leer slope limiters reduce the reconstruction near extrema, even for smooth profiles. Here their integrated absolute errors approach second order, while their RMSE slopes remain near 1.8. The norm matters: a local loss of accuracy near extrema is more prominent in RMSE than in an integrated absolute error. WENO3's fixed smoothing parameter also affects its approach to the asymptotic regime. The CSV files retain every measured error rather than replacing the curves with fitted formal-order lines.

The timing plot compares the same smooth problem at fixed CFL; bands show the three-run range. It separates cost per grid from accuracy per unit work. These results concern this implementation and machine. They are not solver rankings for other models or hardware. The common-CFL error and timing curves include van Leer, WENO3, Centered and LinearUpwind alongside the older choices. The matched Inguva shift is excluded from the C=0.5 curves because that time step violates its contract; its C=1 error and timing remain in the exported CSV files. HOCADI and InguvaBraatz2023 are two-coordinate methods and are assessed in their own examples.

Raw front results are in errors.csv; the smooth results and their measured refinement slopes are in smooth_errors.csv.

Complete runnable script

The code below is read from the example source during the documentation build. Run it from the repository root using the environment described above.

examples/constant_growth_methods.jl

module ConstantGrowthMethods
using PopulationBalanceModels
using Statistics

const growth=0.1
initial_density(a)=a>=0 ? 100exp(-a/0.01) : 0.0
primitive(a)=a<=0 ? 0.0 : -expm1(-a/0.01)
# Difference of two nearly saturated CDF values loses the exponential tail.
function exact_counts(grid,t)
    [b<=growth*t ? 0.0 : exp(-max(a-growth*t,0)/0.01)*
        (-expm1(-(b-max(a,growth*t))/0.01))
        for (a,b) in zip(grid.edges[1:end-1],grid.edges[2:end])]
end
function smooth_counts(grid,t)
    a=grid.edges[1:end-1].-growth*t;b=grid.edges[2:end].-growth*t
    grid.widths.+0.2.*(cos.(2pi.*a).-cos.(2pi.*b))./(2pi).+
        0.1.*(sin.(4pi.*b).-sin.(4pi.*a))./(4pi)
end

function combinations()
    [("upwind","euler",Upwind(),ForwardEuler(),1,1),
     ("upwind","ssprk22",Upwind(),SSPRK22(),1,2),
     ("upwind","ssprk33",Upwind(),SSPRK33(),1,3),
     ("upwind","rk4",Upwind(),RK4(),1,4),
     ("muscl","ssprk33",MUSCL(),SSPRK33(),2,3),
     ("muscl","rk4",MUSCL(),RK4(),2,4),
     ("vanleer","rk4",VanLeer(),RK4(),2,4),
     ("weno3","rk4",WENO3(),RK4(),3,4),
     ("weno5","ssprk33",WENO5(),SSPRK33(),5,3),
     ("weno5","rk4",WENO5(),RK4(),5,4),
     ("weno5","ssprk54",WENO5(),SSPRK54(),5,4),
     ("centered","rk4",Centered(),RK4(),2,4),
     ("linear_upwind","rk4",LinearUpwind(),RK4(),2,4),
     ("lax_wendroff","coupled",nothing,LaxWendroff(),2,2),
     ("beam_warming","coupled",nothing,BeamWarming(),2,2),
     ("inguva2022","coupled",nothing,Inguva2022(),1,1)]
end

function measure(grid,initial,truth,scheme,time,tf,dt,bc;repeats=3)
    model=PBMModel(growth=Growth(AffineGrowth(growth,0.0)))
    operator=scheme===nothing ? compile(model,grid;evolution=time,bc) : compile(model,grid;growth_scheme=scheme,bc)
    problem=PBMProblem(operator,initial,(0.0,tf))
    simulate(problem;method=time,dt,check_nonnegative=false) # compilation/warmup
    elapsed=Float64[];solution=nothing
    for _ in 1:repeats
        timed=@timed simulate(problem;method=time,dt,check_nonnegative=false)
        push!(elapsed,timed.time);solution=timed.value
    end
    N=vec(last(solution.u));f=density(grid,N);error=density(grid,N.-truth)
    (;N,f,rmse=sqrt(sum(error.^2 .*grid.widths)/sum(grid.widths)),
        l1=sum(abs.(error).*grid.widths),maximum_error=maximum(abs.(error)),
        minimum_density=minimum(f),negative_number=-sum(min.(N,0)),
        number_drift=sum(N)-sum(initial),seconds=median(elapsed),
        timing_minimum=minimum(elapsed),timing_maximum=maximum(elapsed),steps=solution.steps)
end

function run(;grids=(50,100,200,400,800,1600),smooth_grids=(40,80,160,320,640),
        courants=(0.5,1.0),tf=4.0,repeats=3,
        output=joinpath(@__DIR__,"..","docs","src","assets","constant_growth"))
    mkpath(output);choices=combinations()
    open(joinpath(output,"errors.csv"),"w") do io
        println(io,"scheme,time_method,courant,bins,rmse,mean_absolute_error,max_error,minimum_density,negative_number,number_drift,seconds,steps")
        for n in grids,C in courants,(name,tname,scheme,time,_,_) in choices
            # LinearUpwind/RK4 at CFL=1 is outside its linear stability interval.
            name=="linear_upwind" && C>0.65 && continue
            name=="inguva2022" && C!=1.0 && continue
            grid=Grid1D(range(0,1;length=n+1);coordinate=:length)
            truth=exact_counts(grid,tf)
            result=measure(grid,exact_counts(grid,0),truth,scheme,time,tf,C/(n*growth),
                name=="inguva2022" ? SizeBC(OpenBoundary(),OpenBoundary()) : SizeBC(OpenBoundary(),NoFlux());repeats)
            println(io,join((name,tname,C,n,result.rmse,result.l1,result.maximum_error,
                result.minimum_density,result.negative_number,result.number_drift,result.seconds,result.steps),','))
            if n==200
                open(joinpath(output,"$(name)_$(tname)_$(C)_$(n).csv"),"w") do data
                    println(data,"size,density,exact")
                    for i in eachindex(result.f);println(data,join((grid.centers[i],result.f[i],truth[i]/grid.widths[i]),','));end
                end
            end
        end
    end
    open(joinpath(output,"smooth_errors.csv"),"w") do io
        println(io,"scheme,time_method,step_policy,bins,dt,courant,rmse,l1,observed_rmse_order,seconds,timing_minimum,timing_maximum,steps,minimum_density,number_drift")
        for (name,tname,scheme,time,space_order,time_order) in choices,policy in ("fixed_cfl","spatial")
            name=="inguva2022" && policy=="spatial" && continue
            # Avoid presenting a prohibitively small RK3 step as a timing comparison.
            policy=="spatial" && !(tname in ("euler","rk4","ssprk54","coupled")) && continue
            previous=NaN;previous_n=0
            for n in smooth_grids
                grid=Grid1D(range(0,1;length=n+1);coordinate=:length);h=1/n
                exponent=policy=="fixed_cfl" ? 1.0 : max(1.0,space_order/time_order)
                dt=(name=="inguva2022" ? 1.0 : 0.5)*h^exponent/growth
                result=measure(grid,smooth_counts(grid,0),smooth_counts(grid,tf),scheme,time,tf,dt,
                    SizeBC(Periodic(),Periodic());repeats)
                order=isfinite(previous) ? log(previous/result.rmse)/log(n/previous_n) : NaN
                println(io,join((name,tname,policy,n,dt,growth*dt/h,result.rmse,result.l1,order,
                    result.seconds,result.timing_minimum,result.timing_maximum,result.steps,result.minimum_density,result.number_drift),','))
                previous=result.rmse;previous_n=n
            end
        end
    end
    open(joinpath(output,"inputs.txt"),"w") do io
        println(io,"Paper problem: Inguva et al. (2022), Case 1 Eq.56: g=0.1, f0=100exp(-a/0.01), domain [0,1], tf=",tf)
        println(io,"Zero inflow and closed upper boundary; front is discontinuous. Errors compare analytical bin integrals; no formal smooth order is claimed for this front.")
        println(io,"Smooth order check: f0=1+0.2sin(2πa)+0.1cos(4πa), periodic domain [0,1].")
        println(io,"Fixed-CFL runs use C=0.5. Spatial checks use dt=0.5*h^max(1,p/q)/g for spatial order p and time order q; actual dt/CFL are exported.")
        println(io,"Inguva2022 uses its required C=1 (not the C=.5 common comparison) and paired open boundaries for the front; the outgoing initial tail is negligible at tf=4. Smooth tests are periodic.")
        println(io,"Timings are medians of ",repeats," warmed integrations, excluding compilation/plotting; minima/maxima exported.")
        println(io,"No clipping; signed density minima and number drift are retained. Julia ",VERSION,"; CPU ",Sys.CPU_NAME)
    end
    println("Growth comparison written to ",output)
end
if abspath(PROGRAM_FILE)==@__FILE__;run();end
end
