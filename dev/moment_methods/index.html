Moment methods

Sometimes the quantities of interest are total number, mean size, or total particle material rather than every bin of a distribution. A moment model advances integrals directly:

\[\mu_k(t)=\int_0^\infty x^kn(x,t)\,dx.\]

This differs from calling moment(grid,N,k) on a sectional solution. In a moment model the moments are the dynamical variables. We need equations for their rates, and often an assumption to express those rates using finitely many tracked moments.

Integrate the balance against a particle property

For vanishing unmodelled boundary flux, the growth, source, removal, and collision contributions are

\[\frac{d\mu_k(t)}{dt} =k\int_0^\infty x^{k-1}G(x,t)n(x,t)\,dx+J(t)x_c^k+S_k(t) -\int_0^\infty x^kd(x,t)n(x,t)\,dx+\mathcal A_k(t)+\mathcal F_k(t),\]

with additive-coordinate aggregation

\[\mathcal A_k(t)=\frac12\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty [(x+y)^k-x^k-y^k]K(x,y,t)n(x,t)n(y,t)\,dx\,dy\]

and breakage

\[\mathcal F_k(t)=\int_0^\infty a(x,t)n(x,t) \left[\sum_j\nu_j(x,t)x_j(x,t)^k-x^k\right]dx.\]

For a nonadditive coordinate, the aggregate size is obtained through the declared AdditiveCoordinate map. Boundary losses at zero or infinity are not inferred. In particular, dissolution reaching zero needs an extinction treatment beyond the current raw-moment rules.

Use an exact closure when the equations close

Affine growth and constant-kernel aggregation in an additive coordinate produce a closed hierarchy through any chosen integer order. Declare the polynomial structure so the compiler can find its moment dependencies:

using PopulationBalanceModels
basis = MomentSet(0:3;coordinate=:volume)
model = PBMModel(
    growth=Growth(rate=PolynomialRate(((p,t,ctx)->p.g,0.1))),
    primary=Nucleation(rate=(p,t,ctx)->p.J,size=0.0),
    aggregation=Aggregation(ConstantKernel((p,t,ctx)->p.K)),
)
operator = compile(model,basis;closure=ExactClosure())
problem = PBMProblem(operator,[1.0,1.0,2.0,6.0],(0.0,1.0);
    p=(g=0.2,J=0.1,K=0.3))
solution = simulate(problem;dt=0.001)
vec(solution.u[end])
4-element Vector{Float64}:
  0.9567097695735923
  1.3108134559817026
  3.3919302035987213
 13.101464813188214

ExactClosure also supports polynomial removal, self-similar polynomial-rate breakage, point creation, and MomentSource. Removal and breakage polynomial coefficients must be nonnegative; growth coefficients may be signed. Compilation rejects equations that need untracked moments. A distributed source must provide its raw moments through MomentSource(J,(k,p,t,ctx)->E_xk).

The cooling-crystallization model has an exact four-moment closure because its growth is affine in length. Its build_moments routine uses the same kinetic parameters and nucleation callback as the sectional builder, and couples the third moment to dissolved concentration. This supplies a useful check independent of the sectional discretization.

Approximate an unclosed hierarchy

General size-dependent rates can require integrals that the tracked moments do not determine. The implemented approximation families make different assumptions:

  • QMOM(n) (also QuadratureClosure(n)) reconstructs positive quadrature nodes and weights from moments 0:2n−1. The rate callbacks are evaluated through their weak forms at those nodes. Scaled Hankel inversion is checked by reconstructing the input moments. Empty states are supported; rank-deficient nonempty states fail rather than silently changing node count or repairing moments.
  • LogNormalClosure(quadrature_order=16) fits a lognormal density to moments 0:2 and integrates its weak forms with cached normal quadrature. Empty and point-mass limits are supported. A three-moment assumed density cannot recover multimodality, even if its weak-form quadrature is accurate.

realizability reports necessary Hankel positivity checks. These do not prove realizability at singular truncated-moment boundaries. A closure needs its own accuracy and convergence assessment; selecting QMOM does not establish agreement with a full size distribution for nonlinear nanoparticle kinetics.

Select weak-form rules and couple populations

The reconstruction family and each term's integration rule can be chosen separately. For example:

operator = compile(model,basis;method=QMOM(2),
    methods=TermMethods(growth=ExactMomentRule(),
                        aggregation=QuadratureMomentRule()))

Here polynomial growth is integrated analytically while aggregation uses the reconstructed quadrature. closure= remains available for raw-moment families. The reconstruction is shared within a residual evaluation; term_methods(operator) records the rules used. examples/moment_closures.jl exercises all three families.

Moment populations can share a PBMSystem with 1D sectional populations. MomentExchange(:particles,:c,k;factor=...) derives bulk exchange from the actual moment rate. Its invariant $c+\mathrm{factor}\,\mu_k$ assumes a fixed conversion factor; a changing density or composition needs its additional balance. PopulationTransfer and CrossAggregation currently require sectional populations; moment interactions can be written explicitly through CustomCoupling.

Use MomentObservable, PopulationMoment, or a custom observation to measure the result. Histogram predictions require a declared reconstruction or measurement model. Optim and SciML adapters can use moment problems, subject to the same closure and boundary restrictions.

The extension interface also supports solver states other than raw moments, with explicit maps for moment values and their rates. Its tested direct-state example is an extension contract, not a general DQMOM solver. EQMOM, CQMOM, multivariate moment closures, and maximum-entropy reconstruction remain future implementations.
