Numerical methods

Writing a kinetic law does not determine how it should be discretized. On a grid, growth transports the population through internal-coordinate faces, nucleation creates entities, and a collision removes parents and deposits products. Their rates have different units and their numerical contributions must preserve different quantities. Time integration then advances the sum of those contributions.

The manual treats these operations separately:

Part of the calculationNumerical treatment
Growth and dissolutionSigned face fluxes with Upwind, MUSCL, van Leer, WENO3, WENO5, Centered, or LinearUpwind, subject to grid restrictions
Random-fluctuation GRDSizeDiffusion differences D times density, using conservative centred fluxes
Transport along growth trajectoriesInguva2022 and InguvaBraatz2023 for eligible autonomous growth, with matched meshes and time steps
NucleationBoundary influx, point deposition, or an integrated size distribution
Birth, death, and dilutionBin source integration and nonnegative removal hazards
Aggregation and breakageFixed-pivot event counting and deposition in an additive property
Time evolutionEuler, SSPRK22, SSPRK33, SSPRK54, RK4, optional external ODE solvers, or restricted complete transport updates
Axial flow and dispersionUpwind axial advection and centered dispersive fluxes, assembled with intrinsic mechanisms

Each linked page gives the physical equation, callback units, discrete treatment, boundary assumptions, and restrictions. Alternative collision methods require an implementation through the extension interface; fixed pivot is the supplied sectional algorithm.

Available choices by term

For a one-coordinate sectional model, these are the implemented choices. The default is listed first. Select a mechanism in TermMethods or override a named term with by_term; this does not select a time integrator.

Physical termSupplied methodsConditions
GrowthUpwind, MUSCL, VanLeer, WENO3, WENO5, Centered, LinearUpwindThe first five support fixed nonuniform 1D meshes, including logarithmic meshes. WENO5 needs at least six bins and numerically resolved reconstruction weights. Centered and LinearUpwind need uniform meshes. WENO and unlimited schemes have no positivity guarantee.
SizeDiffusionFiniteVolumeDiffusionThis table's Grid1D case uses closed or paired-periodic SizeBC. For diagonal Grid2D diffusion, callback and boundary scope see the GRD contract.
Nucleation at one sizeFixedPivot, BoundaryInjection, GaussianInjection1D: fixed pivot deposits between pivots; boundary injection needs Growth (inward/zero velocity) or SizeDiffusion (total flux). Gaussian width is in coordinate units, independently of mesh spacing.
Birth, DistributedNucleationMidpointRule, GaussLegendre(n)Callbacks return source density per coordinate; quadrature integrates over each bin.
IntegratedBirth, IntegratedNucleationBinIntegralCallback supplies the bin integral directly, including its physical normalization.
Death, DilutionPointwiseLoss, CellAverageLoss(quadrature=GaussLegendre(n))Nonnegative removal hazard, evaluated at pivots or averaged in each cell.
Aggregation, BreakageFixedPivotEvent counting and additive-coordinate product deposition; error/discard overflow policy. No other sectional collision algorithm is supplied.
CustomTermNativeResidualUser residual and its conservation/validation contract.

Two-coordinate models have a smaller set: Growth2D supports Upwind and WENO5; Birth and DistributedNucleation support midpoint or tensor Gauss quadrature; Death and Dilution support pointwise or cell-average loss; Nucleation2D supports fixed-pivot and bin-integrated Gaussian deposition. WENO5 supports fixed rectangular meshes with uniform or nonuniform axes (at least six bins per axis). Other 1D reconstruction policies, sectional collisions and boundary point injection are not general 2D implementations. Tensor populations can share bulk balances with other populations in PBMSystem. SizeDiffusion((Dx,Dy)) adds diagonal Ito diffusion. Independent Wiener increments do not imply an independent joint population; see the coefficient, boundary and coupling contract.

MomentSet declares the tracked moment basis. Compilation selects a moment method separately from each term's weak-form rule. ExactClosure uses ExactMomentRule for supported polynomial/closed terms. QMOM (QuadratureClosure), RankAdaptiveQMOM, Gamma EQMOM, DQMOM and LogNormalClosure use QuadratureMomentRule by default, with exact rules available for eligible named terms. A closure must provide any missing higher moments; neither a requested moment order nor a quadrature rule creates an unavailable closure. DQMOM evolves positive weights and weighted abscissas; the other families evolve raw moments. See Moment methods for support and realizability restrictions.

For axial reactors, intrinsic terms retain their representation-specific choices, except SizeDiffusion, which currently rejects axial adapters. The single-population adapter uses axial upwind fluxes. The coupled axial_problem adapter also accepts axial_scheme=MUSCL(), WENO3() and other eligible residual reconstructions. Dispersion uses centered gradient fluxes.

The Li supplementary-coefficient audit is a diagnostic implementation, not the new conservative HOCFiniteVolume adaptation. See Compact finite-volume growth for the implemented contract and verification notes for measured orders and literal literature audits. The method pages explain these restrictions beside their equations and boundary treatments.

Select a representation first

Grid1D stores $N_i(t)=\int_{x_{i-1/2}}^{x_{i+1/2}}n(x,t)\,dx$. Grid2D stores numbers over rectangular internal-coordinate bins. Neither stores a pointwise density. MomentSet declares integrals weighted by powers of the coordinate. Raw-moment methods evolve them directly; DQMOM uses weights and weighted abscissas. Their state-to-moment mappings and weak-form choices are described in Moment methods. The compiler checks that a requested treatment exists for the chosen representation.

Select a method for each term

Suppose a model has boundary nucleation and a second creation mechanism at an interior size. Give the sources names, then select their treatments explicitly:

using PopulationBalanceModels

growth_rate(x,p,t,ctx) = p.G
primary_rate(p,t,ctx) = p.J
secondary_rate(p,t,ctx) = p.J/2

model = PBMModel(
    growth = Growth(rate=growth_rate),
    primary = Nucleation(rate=primary_rate,size=0.0),
    secondary = Nucleation(rate=secondary_rate,size=0.8),
)
grid = Grid1D(0.0:0.25:10.0;coordinate=:volume)
methods = TermMethods(
    growth = MUSCL(),
    by_term = (primary=BoundaryInjection(:left),secondary=FixedPivot()),
)
operator = compile(model,grid;methods)
term_methods(operator)
(growth = MUSCL{Float64}(1.5), primary = BoundaryInjection(:left), secondary = FixedPivot(:inherit))

growth=MUSCL() applies to the growth mechanism. Entries in by_term apply to the names declared in this particular model. Selection uses a named override first, a mechanism choice second, and the default method otherwise. Unknown names and unsupported combinations raise errors. The older growth_scheme= keyword remains available; an explicit TermMethods growth choice takes precedence.

Compilation prepares the selected methods. Adding parameters and an initial state produces a problem that can be integrated:

problem = PBMProblem(operator,zeros(length(grid)),(0.0,0.2);p=(G=0.2,J=1.0))
solution = simulate(problem;method=SSPRK33(),dt=0.002)
sum(solution.u[end])
0.29999999999999893

The temporal derivative is supplied by the integrator; it is not declared as a physical PBMModel term. SSPRK33 evaluates and sums all selected residuals at its stages, including any bulk ODEs. It does not apply a separate time update to growth and nucleation.

For an external algorithm, prepare_term validates the term/method combination and prepares its cache; add_term! adds its residual. The extension guide gives the signatures and conservation contract.

Joint moment admissibility

Componentwise positivity does not guarantee realizability of raw moments. The opt-in MeasureEuler policy evolves positive measures and checks closure admissibility before physical rates and at accepted states. Its compatibility matrix distinguishes raw moment families, sectional updates and the conservative low-order axial guarantee.
