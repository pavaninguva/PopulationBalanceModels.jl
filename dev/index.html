PopulationBalanceModels.jl

A population balance describes how a distribution of particles changes through transport in particle properties and through discrete creation, removal, and collision events. PopulationBalanceModels turns those mechanisms into an executable model while keeping physical laws separate from numerical choices.

Begin with Getting started, then define coordinates, units, boundaries, and kinetic laws in Defining models. Coupled systems connect distributions to shared process states. Numerical methods explains how the equations become residuals and time updates; Moment methods covers reduced representations. Observations and estimation maps the same forward model to data.

The case studies focus on analytical two-coordinate transport, coupled cooling crystallization, and lipid nanoparticle formation. Shorter worked examples introduce the model and inference APIs. Read the API reference alongside these explanations. The developer guide covers internals and extensions, and publishing describes versioned documentation hosting.

The active scope is nonspatial population balances. A two-coordinate distribution uses two internal particle properties, not two physical-space coordinates. CFD transport is deferred. Numerical benchmarks test specific equations and configurations; they do not establish general positivity, closure accuracy, or reproduction of the paper examples.
