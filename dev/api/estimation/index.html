Parameter estimation

Bayesian estimation shares one statistical model across MAP, uncertainty and sampling. Direct distributions always declare physical priors; every parameter requires an explicit transform. MAP, local covariance and sampling use the same declared-coordinate density, including physical-prior Jacobians exactly once. Full-Hessian Laplace and sensitivity-based Gauss–Newton approximations have separate assumptions and diagnostics. The older Gaussian-calibration and deterministic-discrepancy interfaces retain their distinct interpretations.

Begin with the complete first MAP fit. The estimation guide covers likelihoods, derivatives, profiles, posterior sampling and prediction budgets. Eligible stationary models use the same interfaces through StationarySolver.

PopulationBalanceModels.diagnostics — Method
diagnostics(z, statistics; min_ess=400, rhat_limit=1.01, bfmi_limit=0.3,
            max_depth=8, quantity_values=NamedTuple(), mcse_limits=NamedTuple())

Reassess retained MCMC draws with the same screen used by sample_posterior. Requires AdvancedHMC and MCMCDiagnosticTools. z has dimensions (parameters, draws, chains) in inference coordinates; statistics contains one matching post-warmup AdvancedHMC statistics vector per chain. At least two chains, finite rank-normalized R-hat/ESS/MCSE, the declared thresholds, adequate energy BFMI, zero divergences and zero depth hits are required to pass.

Optional named quantity_values are finite draws × chains scalar summaries. Each is also screened for R-hat and bulk/tail ESS. mcse_limits can additionally specify absolute mean/quantile Monte Carlo errors in that quantity's units, e.g. (rate=(mean=0.01,quantiles=(0.025=>0.03,0.975=>0.03)),). A scalar limit means a mean-MCSE limit. Constant or unresolved quantities have undefined diagnostics and do not pass. These errors concern estimation of a posterior summary, not posterior width or observation noise. This standalone screen does not apply the sampler's minimum length or consecutive-pass rule.

PopulationBalanceModels.laplace_approximation — Method
laplace_approximation(problem,map;step, hessian_tolerance=0.01,...)

Explicitly requested approximation from the actual negative log posterior, not a likelihood-only/Gauss–Newton substitute. Checks optimizer convergence, stationarity, gradients, independent step-halved verification of the AD z Hessian, positive curvature and condition number. A local check cannot establish Gaussian shape, unimodality or accurate tail coverage.

PopulationBalanceModels.posterior_predictive — Method
posterior_predictive(problem,draws;max_solves,measurement_noise=false,seed=2022)

Propagate an explicit finite set of parameter draws. Budget counts one forward solve per experiment per draw and is checked BEFORE execution. Returns raw predictions, latent measurement means and optionally fresh measurement noise. Multinomial predictions condition on the observed snapshot totals. Contrast Gaussian outputs remain in contrast coordinates; they are not fabricated PSDs. Failures are returned by draw with their messages and counted; no clipping occurs.

PopulationBalanceModels.CalibrationProblem — Type
CalibrationProblem(experiments; parameters=(z,experiment)->z, dt=nothing, solver=nothing)

parameters maps unconstrained optimization coordinates into physical model parameters separately for each experiment, e.g. positive rate = exp(z[1]). Initial conditions/fields differ by experiment. The same parameter vector can therefore be fitted jointly to several experiments and measurement types. Supply dt for SSPRK33, or solver=(problem,times)->solution for an external integrator. A solver must return precisely those output times and must check its own success code. Initial data that depend on fitted parameters require a separate model construction; the parameter map changes p, not u0.

PopulationBalanceModels.objective — Method

Known-covariance Gaussian negative log likelihood, including normalization, for Observation blocks. For ResidualObservation blocks, returns the sum of their weighted group-mean squared discrepancies, without likelihood normalization. The two interpretations cannot be mixed. NumericalFailure alone can optionally be mapped to Inf. Programming/model-definition errors are never silently turned into optimizer penalties.

PopulationBalanceModels.sensitivity_report — Method

Residual Jacobian (ForwardDiff by default) in optimization coordinates, its singular values and numerical rank. Local covariance is (J'J)^(-1) ONLY at full column rank. This local covariance alone specifies neither a confidence level nor structural identifiability. Use confidenceellipsoid with its assumptions and checks, and profilelikelihood to investigate local asymmetry. Select derivatives=:finite_difference explicitly for an incompatible forward model.

PopulationBalanceModels.DifferentiationFailure — Type

PopulationBalanceModels.SciMLSensitivitySolver — Type
SciMLSensitivitySolver(algorithm; sensitivity=:auto, reltol=1e-7, abstol=1e-9, kwargs...)

Optional inference solver enabled by SciMLBase, SciMLSensitivity and ReverseDiff (loaded by SciMLSensitivity). Automatic selection uses continuous forward sensitivities for at most 16 coordinates and an interpolating adjoint otherwise. Explicit :forward/:adjoint choices are available. Includes parameter-dependent initial states and direct likelihood, response and noise dependence. ODE parameters are the full inference vector. Supports differentiable fixed-representation residuals; changing grid topology, direct shifts, eigen-based QMOM inversion and user callbacks may be ineligible. Hessians use nested ForwardDiff through the primal solve, not an unverified second derivative of an adjoint. Finite differences are an explicit alternative.

Local Gaussian in the declared inference coordinates, with precision jacobian' * jacobian + prior_hessian. The Jacobian is of the joint whitened Gaussian residuals. This omits residual-weighted second prediction derivatives; it is distinct from full-Hessian LaplaceApproximation and sampler tuning.

PopulationBalanceModels.GaussNewtonFailure — Type

A rejected sensitivity-based approximation. report retains the failed checks; no ridge, pseudoinverse or replacement uncertainty is inserted.

PopulationBalanceModels.gauss_newton_approximation — Method
gauss_newton_approximation(problem, map; step=cbrt(eps(Float64)), ...)

Construct sensitivity-based posterior covariance at a converged MAP point: P = J'J + ∇²[-logprior(problem,z)], C = P⁻¹. J differentiates the joint whitened residuals after observation responses and any independent contrasts. Every experiment, time and fitted parameter (including nuisance parameters) is included. Prior curvature uses the declared inference density, including physical-prior transformation Jacobians exactly once.

Initially supports fixed scalar/array Gaussian standard deviations or covariance matrices. Parameter-dependent noise callbacks, count likelihoods and truncated Gaussian likelihoods are rejected, rather than omitting their information. ForwardDiff is the default Jacobian backend; derivatives=:finite_difference is explicit. An external ODE adapter must support that prediction-Jacobian path; this function does not silently select an adjoint or a different solver.

Checks MAP stationarity, residual/target gradient consistency, independent step-halved prediction sensitivities and prior curvature, positive joint precision and conditioning. information_tolerance bounds the precision perturbation in its own covariance metric, including weak directions. No jitter or pseudoinverse is used. Data rank deficiency is reported and may be supported by prior curvature; it is not interpreted as identification by the data.

The approximation omits residual-weighted second prediction derivatives. Successful checks establish neither a full-posterior minimum nor Gaussian tails, unimodality or model adequacy. It is not full-Hessian Laplace. Draws transform to physical parameters and report prior/constraint support violations without truncation. No PBM solves are needed to draw parameters; predictive propagation still costs solves. All covariance entries are in inference units.

PopulationBalanceModels.ParameterSpec — Type
ParameterSpec(name; initial, prior, transform, role=:kinetic, experiment=nothing)

One fitted physical scalar. Supply its physical starting guess and a continuous univariate prior, e.g. ParameterSpec(:g0; initial=0.2, prior=LogNormal(log(0.2),0.5), transform=LogTransform()). The guess is neither a prior statistic nor a fixed parameter; it never mutates the base forward problem. Kinetic callbacks receive physical candidate values.

Select coordinates explicitly, including IdentityTransform() for untransformed parameters. Different parameters may use different transforms. A transform cannot remove allowed physical prior support: truncate a Normal explicitly before using log coordinates. Custom support/transform compatibility is the caller's declaration. Inspect parameterization(problem) or spec.transform. Invalid guesses are rejected, never clamped.

MAP, local uncertainty and MCMC use the same declared inference-coordinate density, including each physical-prior Jacobian once. The mapped MAP is not generally the physical-density mode or posterior mean. experiment=nothing shares the scalar; a name makes it local. role=:nuisance labels initial conditions, calibration or noise parameters. Fixed values belong in the estimation problem or experiment's fixed tuple.

PopulationBalanceModels.Prior — Type
Prior(distribution; coordinates)

Compatibility density declaration. In new code pass a continuous univariate distribution directly to ParameterSpec: it is a physical prior. :physical is equivalent. Legacy :inference declarations preserve their tagged law for z and receive no additional parameter Jacobian. PopulationBalanceModels.check_sampling_map — Method
check_sampling_map(problem, map, z; derivatives=problem.derivatives,
                   step=cbrt(eps(Float64)), tolerance=1e-3)

Check a supplied sampling map at an inference-coordinate point z. The named fields are to_sampler, from_sampler, logabsdetjac and label. logabsdetjac(y) means log|det D(fromsampler)(y)|. The sampler density is `logposterior(problem,fromsampler(y)) + logabsdetjac(y)`.

Checks both inverse directions, the Jacobian determinant, and the pulled-back posterior gradient against independent step-halved differences. Repeat at representative points and tails in the ORIGINAL posterior support. Callbacks must be pure and define a bijection covering that entire support; finite tests cannot certify global coverage or injectivity. The sampler also checks inverse and determinant consistency at every mapped target evaluation. No priors or physical parameter transforms are redefined by this auxiliary change.

PopulationBalanceModels.InferenceExperiment — Method
InferenceExperiment(name, problem::StationaryPBMProblem, measurements...;
                    solver=StationarySolver(), setup, fixed=NamedTuple())

One stationary operating point, sharing the transient framework's parameters, priors, likelihoods, responses and source tags. Declare timeless measurements with Measurement(data, observable; likelihood=...). Matrix columns are replicates of the same state. Distinct operating points need distinct experiments. setup(theta, base) must return a stationary problem. A custom one-argument solver must return a converged, checked StationaryPBMSolution for that problem. No timestep or artificial time series is used.

source
PopulationBalanceModels.StationarySolver — Type
StationarySolver([algorithm]; condition_limit=1e12, abstol=1e-11, kwargs...)

Checked stationary forward solver for calibration and Bayesian estimation. Requires the optional NonlinearSolve integration. Remaining keywords pass to solve_stationary. Each evaluation starts from the problem's declared guess; there is no hidden continuation or warm-start history.

ForwardDiff predictions differentiate the equilibrium equations implicitly, not the nonlinear iterations. The equilibrated state Jacobian must be nonsingular with condition number below condition_limit. This local result requires a smooth, isolated root on a consistent branch. Quadrature closures that cannot differentiate their residual must explicitly use derivatives=:finite_difference in the estimation/calibration problem. The current implicit implementation uses dense Jacobians and factorizations.

PopulationBalanceModels.sample_posterior — Function
sample_posterior(problem; stopping=:auto, samples=nothing, warmup=500,
    chains=4, min_samples=500, batch_samples=250, max_samples=10_000,
    consecutive_passes=2, min_ess=400, rhat_limit=1.01, bfmi_limit=0.3, ...)

Load AdvancedHMC and MCMCDiagnosticTools for optional NUTS. Sampling is never automatic in fit. With no samples count, the default runs warmup once per chain, freezes adaptation and continues the same chains in batches. At least two chains are required. Once the minimum retained length is reached, the rank-normalized R-hat, bulk/tail ESS, energy BFMI and finite-MCSE screen must pass at consecutive_passes successive checkpoints. Draw counts are per chain. The budget is max_samples; this is not a wall-time or forward-solve budget.

An explicit samples=N preserves fixed-length behavior. Equivalently select stopping=:fixed, samples=N; single-chain runs remain available in that mode. stopping=:diagnostics explicitly selects the controller and rejects samples; use min_samples/max_samples instead. A fixed run still reports failed checks.

diagnostics(result) records stopping_policy, stopping_reason, actual samples, and check_history. Reasons are :diagnostics_passed, :max_samples, :sampler_pathology or :fixed_length. Diagnostic mode stops unsuccessfully when a retained divergence/depth hit makes its zero-tolerance screen impossible to pass by appending draws. No bad draw is discarded and no kernel is silently retuned. A final single-check screen_passed does not satisfy an unfinished consecutive-pass rule: diagnostics_passed is false on budget exhaustion.

Optional diagnostic_quantities=(rate=theta -> theta.shared.rate,...) supplies deterministic finite scalar functions of the physical (shared,experiments) parameter tuple, without additional PBM solves. These quantities also enter the R-hat/ESS screen. Absolute precision requirements can be supplied as mcse_limits=(rate=(mean=0.01,quantiles=(0.025=>0.03,0.975=>0.03)),); a scalar limit means mean MCSE only. Limits have the quantity's physical units. Quantile MCSE concerns uncertainty in an estimated interval endpoint, not the posterior interval width. Constant/unresolved diagnostics cannot pass. These sequential checks are a computational stopping policy, not proof of global mixing, a model-validation test or a rigorous sequential-coverage guarantee.

initial_covariance=C optionally initializes the HMC inverse mass matrix in sampling coordinates (inference coordinates by default); C must be finite, symmetric positive definite and is used only for tuning, not returned as a posterior uncertainty estimate. A tuple/vector of one such matrix per chain allows different initial tuning, with independent adaptation. Neither form can be combined with laplace initialization. A supplied Laplace covariance already uses inference coordinates and is used directly. Only an additional auxiliary sampling map requires a local Jacobian conversion for tuning; that conversion is not a new posterior approximation. An optional sampling_map=(to_sampler,from_sampler,logabsdetjac,label) changes only auxiliary sampler coordinates; logabsdetjac is the log absolute determinant of the Jacobian of fromsampler. Inverses and Jacobians are checked at every mapped target evaluation; transformed gradients are independently checked at chain starts. Use checksamplingmap at additional representative inference-coordinate points, including tails. Returned z values and statistics.logdensity stay in declared inference coordinates; statistics.samplerlogdensity and Hamiltonian energies retain sampler coordinates.

PopulationBalanceModels.confidence_ellipsoid — Method
confidence_ellipsoid(calibration, estimate; level=0.95, variance=:known, ...)

Local Gauss–Newton joint region (z-ẑ)' inv(C) (z-ẑ) <= cutoff. Known absolute errors use chi-square(p); relative weights with an estimated common variance use p*F(p,n-p). Rejects rank deficiency, a nonstationary fit, unverified derivatives, and a nonconverged FitResult. If a refined calibration is supplied, also checks forward and derivative convergence. A nonlinear parameter transform maps the region to a generally nonellipsoidal physical region. The approximation is not a global coverage guarantee.

PopulationBalanceModels.fit_diagnostics — Method
fit_diagnostics(calibration, estimate; variance=:known, refined=nothing, ...)

Assess a fit in its optimization coordinates. Returns whitened residuals, Gauss–Newton covariance/correlation, rank, residual degrees of freedom, stationarity, and agreement with an independent central-difference check. variance=:known uses the supplied absolute observation errors; :estimated treats them as relative weights with one unknown common variance, estimated as RSS/(n-rank).

For a stationary full-rank fit with known errors, the chi-square goodness-of-fit p-value uses n-p degrees of freedom (a local nonlinear approximation). refined may supply a finer forward calculation for the same measurements; the report measures its changes in whitened predictions and derivatives. These diagnostics do not establish model validity or global identifiability.
