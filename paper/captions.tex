\newcommand{\GrowthProfilesCaption}{%
Translation of an initially exponential population, $f(a,0)=100\exp(-a/0.01)$,
at $t=4\,\mathrm{s}$ with $G=0.1$ on $a\in[0,1]$, zero inflow and open outflow.
Both panels use exactly 100 uniform bins; (b) enlarges the translating front.
The reference is the analytical bin average, and the numerical curves are
piecewise-constant densities obtained from bin-integrated populations.
All residual methods and the conservative HOC/midpoint update use $C=0.5$;
Inguva2022 uses its required native $C=1$ update. Sparse markers identify
computed bin values. Signed undershoots are retained, without clipping.
The sharp front is a resolution diagnostic, not a smooth-order experiment.}

\newcommand{\GrowthConvergenceCaption}{%
Smooth periodic constant-growth test on $[0,1]$, with
$f(a,0)=1+0.2\sin(2\pi a)+0.1\cos(4\pi a)$, $G=0.1$ and $t=4\,\mathrm{s}$.
Initial and exact final bin populations are integrated analytically.
Meshes have 40, 80, 160, 320, 640, 1280 and 2560 bins.
(a,b) Integrated $L^1$ error and width-weighted RMSE;
(c,d) corresponding successive-grid orders
$p_{\mathrm{obs}}=\log(E_c/E_f)/\log(N_f/N_c)$.
RMSE is $[\sum_i\Delta a_i(\bar f_i-\bar f_i^{\mathrm{exact}})^2/
\sum_i\Delta a_i]^{1/2}$. Courant numbers are 0.5, except the characteristic
update's required value of one. Orders below the declared roundoff-resolution
threshold are undefined, particularly for nearly exact characteristic shifts.
Finite-range WENO slopes depend on nonlinear weights, temporal error and
roundoff and are not new formal-order estimates. The complete HOC/midpoint method
is second order in time despite its fourth-order interior spatial construction.}

\newcommand{\MomentMethodsCaption}{%
Aggregation--fragmentation of an initially exponential volume distribution
(Qamar problem 4), with additive particle property $x^3$, constant aggregation
kernel $\beta_0=1$, fragmentation hazard $a_0x^3$ with $a_0=1$, and uniform
volume daughters, $b(x\mid y)=6x^2/y^3$. The exact initial number and material
are $N_0=1$ and $V_0=10^{-3}$.
The length density is $n(x,0)=3N_0^2x^2V_0^{-1}
\exp(-N_0x^3/V_0)$.
(a) Number normalized by its initial value; (b,c) percent absolute relative
errors in $\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$ against the analytical solution; (d) material
ratio $\mu_3/V_0$. The number obeys
$d\mu_0/dt=-\beta_0\mu_0^2/2+a_0\mu_3$ and exact material is constant.
QMOM and rank-adaptive QMOM use three nodes; the active adaptive rank remains
three at the saved times, so overlap is expected and does not demonstrate a rank change.
Gamma EQMOM uses three primary kernels with 12 secondary nodes per kernel;
DQMOM uses three weights and three weighted abscissas. Material is evolved for
the raw-moment methods and evaluated directly from DQMOM nodes and weights.
The assumed-lognormal
comparator evolves only three moments and uses 64-point quadrature. Its third
moment is reconstructed: its initial mismatch and subsequent drift are retained.
All use SSPRK33 with maximum step 0.02 s and saves every 0.5 s;
halved steps and four-node QMOM are
reported separately. Sparse markers lie at actual saved times. Errors at or
below $1000\epsilon_{\mathrm{Float64}}$ relative
($2.22\times10^{-11}\,\%$) are omitted from the logarithmic error panels,
not floored; all signed values remain in the tables. This removes initial
roundoff artifacts. Sharp absolute-error dips can accompany signed zero crossings.
Closure error, integration error and unequal representation work are distinct.}

\newcommand{\PrecipitationFieldsCaption}{%
Transient number density in an axially dispersed continuous precipitator,
using Zhang et al. (2024), Case 7A, SI Table S8.
(a--d) $t=5,40,80,200\,\mathrm{s}$ in chronological order.
Actual logarithmic size edges span $1$--$900\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ and the axial
mesh is uniform over $0$--$0.47\,\mathrm{m}$. The displayed numerical reference
has 400 size bins and 400 axial cells. The cell averages are
computed with geometry-aware size WENO5, axial MUSCL, and checked CVODE-BDF;
they are not CADET WENO35 results. CVODE uses maximum BDF order three through
5 s, then restarts at maximum order two from the unchanged physical state,
relative tolerance $10^{-7}$, absolute tolerances $10^{-14}$ for the
$10^{12}$-scaled bin populations and $10^{-11}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ for solute,
and a 0.5 s step cap. All panels share one logarithmic color scale.
Nonpositive cells are masked; values below the displayed range use its lower
color, and signed minima and negative populations are exported. No positive
startup background, image smoothing or spatial interpolation is introduced.
This external integrator has no positivity guarantee; accepted states are checked.
The initial population and solute are zero. Primary and secondary nucleation,
solute depletion, Danckwerts inflow and zero-gradient outflow are retained.
Independent $200\times400$ and $400\times200$ checks separate size and axial
effects. A same-mesh run tightens all tolerances tenfold and halves the step cap;
differences are recorded at all four times. No
analytical coupled PSD solution exists for this case.}
