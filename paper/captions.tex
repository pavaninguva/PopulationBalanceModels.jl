\newcommand{\GrowthProfilesCaption}{%
Translation of the Gaussian $f(a,0)=100\exp[-(a-0.2)^2/(2(0.03)^2)]$
initially restricted to $0\le a\le1$, with $G=0.1$ and $t=4\,\mathrm{s}$.

\newcommand{\MomentMethodsCaption}{%
Aggregation--fragmentation of an initially exponential volume distribution
(Qamar problem 4), with additive particle property $x^3$, constant aggregation
kernel $\beta_0=1$, fragmentation hazard $a_0x^3$ with $a_0=1$, and uniform
volume daughters, $b(x\mid y)=6x^2/y^3$. The exact initial number and material
are $N_0=1$ and $V_0=10^{-3}$.
The length density is $n(x,0)=3N_0^2x^2V_0^{-1}
\exp(-N_0x^3/V_0)$.
(a) Number normalized by its initial value; (b,c) percent absolute relative
errors in $\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$ against the analytical solution; (d) material
ratio $\mu_3/V_0$. The number obeys
$d\mu_0/dt=-\beta_0\mu_0^2/2+a_0\mu_3$ and exact material is constant.
QMOM and rank-adaptive QMOM use three nodes; the active adaptive rank remains
three at the saved times, so overlap is expected and does not demonstrate a rank change.
Gamma EQMOM uses three primary kernels with 12 secondary nodes per kernel;
DQMOM uses three weights and three weighted abscissas. Material is evolved for
the raw-moment methods and evaluated directly from DQMOM nodes and weights.
The assumed-lognormal
comparator evolves only three moments and uses 64-point quadrature. Its third
moment is reconstructed: its initial mismatch and subsequent drift are retained.
All use SSPRK33 with maximum step 0.02 s and saves every 0.5 s;
halved steps and four-node QMOM are
reported separately. Sparse markers lie at actual saved times. Errors at or
below $1000\epsilon_{\mathrm{Float64}}$ relative
($2.22\times10^{-11}\,\%$) are omitted from the logarithmic error panels,
not floored; all signed values remain in the tables. This removes initial
roundoff artifacts. Sharp absolute-error dips can accompany signed zero crossings.
Closure error, integration error and unequal representation work are distinct.}

(a--d) $t=5,40,80,200\,\mathrm{s}$ in chronological order.

stability-limit detection and no prescribed restart,
relative tolerance $10^{-7}$, absolute tolerances $10^{-14}$ for the

and a 0.5 s step cap. All panels share one linear color scale with the

The particle-size axis is logarithmic; densities remain per linear size,
not per log-size. Curves at axial counts 50, 100, 200 and 400 use 1000

\newcommand{\PrecipitationRefinementCaption}{%
Separate refinement at $t=200\,\mathrm{s}$ against the common logarithmic-size
$1000\times500$ numerical reference. (a) Size counts 50, 100, 200 and 400 at
$N_z=500$; (b) axial counts 50, 100, 200 and 400 at $N_x=1000$.
Whole-field and outlet differences integrate the absolute difference of
piecewise-constant number densities over common partitions, divided by the
reference number integral on the corresponding domain. This differs from the
paper's spline comparison. Differences include representation, source-deposition,
evolution and finite-reference errors, not an isolated WENO order.
Both refinements reduce the outlet discrepancy; coarse axial grids give the
larger difference over these tested ranges. Near-overlapping squares and circles
are separately evaluated whole-field and outlet norms.}
